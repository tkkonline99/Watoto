## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 4 Technology & Logic year, the final band in this curriculum, building on Grade 3's conditionals and computational thinking.

### Unit 1: Variables & Advanced Block Coding (Weeks 1–9)

- Understand that a variable stores information that can change
- Use variables to keep score or track values in a program
- Combine variables with loops and conditionals
- Begin transitioning toward simple text-based coding concepts
Key vocabulary: variable, value, score, program, text-based

### Unit 2: Algorithms & Efficiency (Weeks 10–18)

- Write clear step-by-step algorithms to solve problems
- Compare two algorithms and decide which is more efficient
- Understand that there can be more than one solution to a problem
- Refine an algorithm to remove unnecessary steps
Key vocabulary: algorithm, efficient, steps, solution, refine

### Unit 3: How the Internet Works (Weeks 19–27)

- Understand that the internet is a network connecting computers worldwide
- Understand basic terms (website, browser, server, data)
- Understand how information travels across the internet (simply)
- Recognize that data is sent in small pieces and reassembled
Key vocabulary: internet, network, server, browser, data

### Unit 4: Digital Citizenship & Media Literacy (Weeks 28–36)

- Evaluate whether online sources and information are reliable
- Understand digital footprints and long-term consequences of online actions
- Recognize misinformation and how to check facts
- Practice safe, ethical, and responsible technology use
Key vocabulary: reliable, digital footprint, misinformation, media literacy

## 3. Unit 1: Variables & Advanced Block Coding


### Lesson Plan — “Keeping Track with Variables”


**Objective**
Students will understand variables and use them to track values like a score in a program.

**Materials**
Block-coding app or unplugged cards including a 'variable' block, a simple game example.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: 'In a game, how does the computer remember your score as it goes up?'

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce a variable as a container that stores information that can change (like a score). Model creating a score variable and increasing it with a loop and conditional. Mention how text-based code writes similar ideas as words.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children plan a simple program that uses a variable to keep score.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why is a variable useful in a program?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the concept of a score variable. Stretch: use two variables (score and lives) together.

## 4. Unit 2: Algorithms & Efficiency


### Lesson Plan — “Better, Faster Algorithms”


**Objective**
Students will write algorithms and compare them to decide which is more efficient.

**Materials**
Task cards, algorithm-writing templates, two example algorithms for one task.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show two sets of directions to the same place (one longer, one shorter); ask which is better and why.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Review that an algorithm is a step-by-step solution. Show two algorithms for the same task and compare their efficiency (fewer steps, less repetition). Discuss that problems can have more than one solution.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children write an algorithm for a task, then try to remove unnecessary steps.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What makes one algorithm more efficient than another?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on writing a clear algorithm. Stretch: rewrite an inefficient algorithm to make it shorter.

## 5. Unit 3: How the Internet Works


### Lesson Plan — “The Internet Explained”


**Objective**
Students will understand that the internet is a network connecting computers and how information travels.

**Materials**
Simple network diagram, examples of websites/browsers, a 'data in packets' illustration.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: 'When you visit a website, where does the information actually come from?'

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Explain that the internet is a giant network connecting computers worldwide. Introduce basic terms: website, browser, server, data. Explain simply that information is broken into small pieces (packets), sent, and reassembled.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children label a simple diagram of how a web page reaches their device.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why is it useful that data is sent in small pieces?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on network, website, and browser. Stretch: explain the packet idea in their own words.

## 6. Unit 4: Digital Citizenship & Media Literacy


### Lesson Plan — “Thinking Critically Online”


**Objective**
Students will evaluate whether online information is reliable and recognize misinformation.

**Materials**
Examples of reliable and unreliable sources, a fact-checking checklist, digital footprint examples.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: 'If you read something online, how do you know if it's true?'

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Discuss evaluating online sources: who wrote it, is it checked, does it match other sources. Introduce misinformation and how to fact-check. Review digital footprints and long-term consequences. Emphasize safe, ethical, responsible tech use.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children evaluate a few sample 'sources' for reliability.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What are some signs that online information might not be reliable?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the reliability checklist. Stretch: fact-check a claim using more than one source and explain the process.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 4 Technology & Logic year (all 4 units) — the final band in the curriculum.

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Uses variables to track values and combines them with loops and conditionals
- Writes and compares algorithms for efficiency
- Understands what the internet is and how information travels
- Evaluates online information for reliability and understands digital footprints

**Question Types to Expect**
Variable tracking, algorithm writing and efficiency comparison, internet concepts, and media literacy/reliability evaluation.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Explore coding platforms that use variables and text-based coding together
- Compare different ways to solve everyday problems and discuss efficiency
- Talk about how the internet works when using it together
- Practice fact-checking and discuss reliable vs. unreliable sources

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
