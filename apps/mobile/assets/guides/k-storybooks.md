# STORYBOOKS — KINDERGARTEN (AGES 5–6)

Reading Level Guide
Master Curriculum Project
Storybooks is treated as a levelled reading library rather than a tested skills subject — there is no worksheet, quiz, or exam guide here, just reading-level guidance and book categories. It's designed to be used alongside the Reading & Writing Kindergarten package, especially Unit 2 (Blending & Decoding) and Unit 4 (Stories & Retelling).

## Reading Level for This Age

Kindergarten sits at the early emergent reader stage — roughly Guided Reading Level A–C (or Lexile BR, “Beginning Reader”). At this level, books typically feature:
- Simple, predictable sentence patterns, often repeating a structure with one changing word
- Strong picture-to-text match, where the pictures directly support the words
- Familiar, everyday topics: family, animals, school, feelings
- Decodable text using the phonics patterns being learned (especially useful alongside CVC word work)
- A small, growing set of sight words repeated often

## Book Categories & Anchors


### Predictable & Repetitive Picture Books

Books built around a repeating sentence pattern that invites the child to join in or predict the next line. A well-known anchor: “Brown Bear, Brown Bear, What Do You See?” by Bill Martin Jr.

### Decodable Early Readers

Leveled reader series built around specific phonics patterns (short vowels, CVC words, common digraphs). Most reading programs and libraries organize these by level — look for anything labeled “Level A,” “Guided Reading A–C,” or “Emergent Reader.”

### Rhyming Read-Alouds

Playful, rhythmic books that build phonological awareness through rhyme. Early Dr. Seuss titles are a well-known anchor for this category.

### Concept Books That Reinforce Other Subjects

- Alphabet and counting books (ties into Reading & Writing Unit 1 and Math Unit 1)
- Books about the five senses or seasons (ties into Science Units 2–3)
- Books about maps, communities, or family (ties into Social Studies Units 1 and 3)

### Diverse Family & Community Stories

Books representing a range of family structures, cultures, and traditions, tying directly into Social Studies Unit 4 (Celebrating Our Differences).

## How to Use This List

Aim for a mix of two reading modes each day:
- Read-aloud time: an adult reads a book slightly above the child's independent level, modeling fluent, expressive reading
- Shared/independent time: the child reads a decodable, on-level book with support, sounding out words using phonics skills from Reading & Writing
15–20 minutes of daily reading time, split between the two modes, is a strong target for this age.

## Light Comprehension Check-Ins

Not formal tests — just a few questions after reading to build the retelling skills covered in Reading & Writing Unit 4:
- “Who was in the story?”
- “Where did it happen?”
- “What happened at the beginning? At the end?”
- “What was your favorite part, and why?”
