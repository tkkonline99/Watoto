## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Science |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 5–6 (Kindergarten) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Hands-on observation and simple guided experiments; questions and curiosity are treated as the goal, not just correct answers |
| Standards Alignment | US: NGSS K-LS1 / K-ESS2-3 / K-PS2  ·  Canada (ON): Grade K Science & Technology strands  ·  Global: Cambridge Primary Science Stage 1 |
| Assessment Approach | Worksheet → quiz → unit test per unit, plus one cumulative exam guide for parents/teachers |


## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Kindergarten Science year.

### Unit 1: Living Things & Habitats (Weeks 1–9)

- Sort objects into living and non-living
- Identify basic needs of living things (food, water, air, shelter)
- Name examples of animal habitats (pond, forest, ocean, desert)
- Describe how a habitat meets an animal's needs
Key vocabulary: living, non-living, habitat, needs, shelter

### Unit 2: Weather & Seasons (Weeks 10–18)

- Identify the four seasons and their basic characteristics
- Describe daily weather using simple vocabulary (sunny, rainy, windy, snowy, cloudy)
- Match clothing and activities to weather and seasons
- Record simple weather observations over time
Key vocabulary: season, weather, temperature, sunny, rainy, windy, snowy

### Unit 3: My Five Senses (Weeks 19–27)

- Name the five senses and their related body part
- Use each sense to describe and explore objects
- Sort objects or experiences by which sense they involve
- Understand that senses help us learn about the world
Key vocabulary: see, hear, smell, taste, touch, senses

### Unit 4: Push, Pull & Motion (Weeks 28–36)

- Identify examples of pushes and pulls
- Describe how pushes and pulls change an object's motion
- Predict what will happen when a push or pull is applied
- Conduct a simple push/pull investigation
Key vocabulary: push, pull, motion, force, fast, slow

## 3. Unit 1: Living Things & Habitats


### Lesson Plan — “Living or Not Living?”


**Objective**
Students will sort objects into living and non-living categories and explain their reasoning using basic needs.

**Materials**
Picture cards of living things (dog, tree, child, bird, fish) and non-living things (rock, chair, car, ball), two sorting mats labeled “Living” and “Not Living.”

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What do you need to stay alive?” List answers (food, water, air).

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Hold up picture cards one at a time; as a class, decide if each is living or non-living and explain why (Does it grow? Does it need food?).

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children sort a new set of picture cards into living/non-living mats.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask children to name one living thing and one non-living thing they can see in the room right now.

**Differentiation**
Support: use very familiar objects (pets, toys) with obvious answers. Stretch: introduce trickier examples (a seed, a stuffed animal) and discuss why they might be confusing.

## 4. Unit 2: Weather & Seasons


### Lesson Plan — “What's the Weather Today?”


**Objective**
Students will describe daily weather using appropriate vocabulary and identify characteristics of the four seasons.

**Materials**
Weather picture cards (sunny, rainy, windy, snowy, cloudy), four season pictures, a classroom weather chart.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Look outside together and ask: “What's the weather like today?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce weather vocabulary with picture cards; match each to appropriate clothing (umbrella for rainy, mittens for snowy).

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, add today's weather to the classroom weather chart; discuss which season it likely is and why.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask what season it is now and one thing they notice about it (leaves changing, snow, flowers).

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on just 2 weather types and 2 seasons initially. Stretch: keep a simple weather journal for a week.

## 5. Unit 3: My Five Senses


### Lesson Plan — “Exploring With Our Senses”


**Objective**
Students will name the five senses and use them to describe and explore objects.

**Materials**
A “mystery bag” with a few safe, distinct objects (cotton ball, bell, lemon slice, cracker), five senses picture cards.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask children to name a body part they use to see, hear, smell, taste, and touch.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Pass around the mystery bag; children reach in (without looking) and describe what they feel before guessing the object. Discuss which sense they used.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
Set up simple sensory stations (something to smell, something to listen to, something to touch) and rotate through them, naming the sense used at each.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Which sense would you use to know if soup is too hot?” Discuss a few similar examples.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on matching senses to body parts only. Stretch: discuss how senses can work together (e.g., smell affects taste).

## 6. Unit 4: Push, Pull & Motion


### Lesson Plan — “Push It, Pull It!”


**Objective**
Students will identify examples of pushes and pulls and describe how they change an object's motion.

**Materials**
A ball, a wagon or toy car, a rubber band, various classroom objects, a simple ramp (book or board).

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask children to push their hands together, then pull them apart. Discuss what push and pull mean.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Demonstrate pushing a ball to make it roll and pulling a toy car with a string. Ask: “Was that a push or a pull? What happened to the object?”

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
Set up stations (rolling a ball, stretching a rubber band, opening/closing a door) where children identify push or pull and describe the resulting motion.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask children to name one push and one pull they did today.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on very obvious push/pull actions. Stretch: introduce the idea that a bigger push or pull makes something move faster or farther, and test it with a ramp.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

Keep this low-stress and playful — short sessions, hands-on exploring, and no timed pressure. This guide covers the full Kindergarten Science year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Sorts objects into living and non-living, explaining basic needs
- Names the four seasons and matches weather/clothing appropriately
- Identifies the five senses and their body parts
- Identifies examples of pushes and pulls and their effects on motion

**Question Types to Expect**
Sorting/categorizing, matching, fill-in-the-blank, true/false, simple drawing with labels, and short explanations.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Get outside and explore real weather and nature together
- Let kids use their senses to explore safely at home
- Turn everyday moments (opening doors, playing with toys) into push/pull conversations
- Celebrate curiosity and ‘why’ questions rather than rushing to the answer

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
