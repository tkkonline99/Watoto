## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 1 Technology & Logic year, moving from unplugged Kindergarten sequencing into first real coding tools.

### Unit 1: Intro to Block Coding (Weeks 1–9)

- Understand that programs are made of sequences of commands
- Arrange simple block-based commands to move a character or sprite
- Predict what a short sequence of blocks will do before running it
- Identify and fix a simple error in a block sequence
Key vocabulary: program, command, block, sequence, sprite

### Unit 2: Logic Puzzles & Computational Thinking (Weeks 10–18)

- Solve simple logic puzzles (odd one out, what comes next, simple mazes)
- Break a task into smaller steps (decomposition)
- Recognize simple patterns in problems
- Use if/then thinking in everyday situations
Key vocabulary: logic, decompose, pattern, if/then

### Unit 3: Typing & Keyboarding Basics (Weeks 19–27)

- Identify letter locations on a keyboard, with a home-row focus
- Type simple words with correct finger placement (introductory level)
- Practice basic keys: space, enter, backspace
- Build typing comfort and accuracy over speed
Key vocabulary: keyboard, home row, keys, type

### Unit 4: Internet Safety Essentials (Weeks 28–36)

- Reinforce the ask-a-grown-up-first rule for new websites and apps
- Recognize strong ‘keep it private’ habits (not sharing passwords or personal info)
- Identify examples of being kind online
- Know what to do if they see something online that upsets them
Key vocabulary: password, private, kind, digital citizen

## 3. Unit 1: Intro to Block Coding


### Lesson Plan — “My First Block Program”


**Objective**
Students will arrange simple block-based commands to move a character and predict what a sequence will do.

**Materials**
A block-coding app or unplugged block cards (‘move forward,’ ‘turn left,’ ‘turn right’), a simple grid or maze printout.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Review directional words from Kindergarten (forward, turn) using movement.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce block commands (as physical cards or on a coding app if available) and model building a short sequence to move a character to a goal on a grid.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children build their own short block sequences to solve a simple maze, predicting the outcome before testing it.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What happened when the blocks were in the wrong order?”

**Differentiation**
Support: use very short 2–3 block sequences with a simple, large grid. Stretch: introduce a ‘repeat’ block concept for sequences with repeated moves.

## 4. Unit 2: Logic Puzzles & Computational Thinking


### Lesson Plan — “Puzzle It Out”


**Objective**
Students will solve simple logic puzzles and practice breaking a task into smaller steps.

**Materials**
Simple logic puzzle worksheets (odd one out, what comes next, simple mazes), a ‘steps to make a sandwich’ style decomposition activity.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show 3 objects where 2 share something in common; ask which one doesn't belong and why.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Model breaking a familiar task (making a sandwich, getting dressed) into small ordered steps. Discuss why breaking big tasks into small steps helps.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children break down a new simple task into steps together.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What is a big task you did today that had lots of small steps?”

**Differentiation**
Support: use very familiar 3-step tasks. Stretch: introduce simple if/then statements, like ‘If it's raining, then I bring an umbrella.’

## 5. Unit 3: Typing & Keyboarding Basics


### Lesson Plan — “Getting to Know the Keyboard”


**Objective**
Students will identify letter locations on a keyboard and practice typing simple words with growing comfort.

**Materials**
A keyboard (real or printed diagram), simple word cards, a typing practice sheet or app if available.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a keyboard; ask children what they notice about how the letters are arranged.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the ‘home row’ concept (resting fingers on ASDF and JKL;) and practice finding a few letters.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children take turns finding letters called out by a partner on a keyboard or diagram.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask children to type or point to their first name's first letter.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on finding just a few very familiar letters, like those in their name. Stretch: practice typing a short simple word using correct home-row fingers.

## 6. Unit 4: Internet Safety Essentials


### Lesson Plan — “Being Safe and Kind Online”


**Objective**
Students will reinforce the ask-a-grown-up rule, practice keeping information private, and identify examples of being kind online.

**Materials**
Simple scenario cards (online situations), a ‘kind or unkind’ sorting activity.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Review: “What's the rule before using a new app or website?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the idea of a ‘password’ as a private key that should never be shared, even with friends. Discuss what makes a message ‘kind’ vs. ‘unkind’ online, using simple examples.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
Sort scenario cards into ‘kind’ and ‘unkind’ as a class, discussing each one.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What should you do if you see something online that upsets you?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the core rules only — ask first, keep passwords private, be kind, tell a grown-up if upset. Stretch: role-play a few more nuanced online scenarios.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 1 Technology & Logic year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Arranges block commands into working sequences and debugs simple errors
- Solves basic logic puzzles and breaks tasks into smaller steps
- Locates keys on a keyboard and types simple words with growing comfort
- Follows core online safety and kindness rules

**Question Types to Expect**
Sequencing and ordering, pattern completion, matching, true/false, short typing or keyboard tasks, and simple explanations.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Try free beginner coding games together
- Turn daily routines into ‘steps’ conversations
- Practice typing in short, low-pressure sessions
- Reinforce password privacy and online kindness consistently

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
