## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Technology & Logic |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 5–6 (Kindergarten) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Mostly unplugged — movement, games, and puzzles build sequencing and logic before any device is introduced |

## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Kindergarten Technology & Logic year, almost entirely unplugged at this age.

### Unit 1: Sequences & Patterns (Weeks 1–9)

- Recognize, extend, and create simple repeating patterns (AB, ABC)
- Put a short sequence of steps in the correct order
- Follow a 2–3 step direction sequence
- Understand that order matters in a sequence
Key vocabulary: pattern, sequence, order, first, next, last

### Unit 2: Unplugged Coding Basics (Weeks 10–18)

- Give and follow simple step-by-step instructions (an “algorithm”) to complete a task
- Use directional words (forward, backward, turn) to move through a simple unplugged maze
- Identify and fix a simple error in a sequence of steps (“debug”)
- Understand that a “loop” means repeating steps
Key vocabulary: algorithm, instructions, debug, loop, direction

### Unit 3: Meet the Computer (Weeks 19–27)

- Identify basic parts of a computer (screen, keyboard, mouse)
- Practice basic mouse skills (click, drag) and keyboard awareness
- Understand that computers follow instructions given by people
- Recognize computers and devices in everyday life
Key vocabulary: computer, keyboard, mouse, screen, click

### Unit 4: Staying Safe Online (Weeks 28–36)

- Understand the rule of asking a grown-up before using a device or app
- Recognize that some information should stay private (name, address)
- Identify what to do if something online feels confusing or uncomfortable
- Practice basic screen-time awareness
Key vocabulary: private, safe, grown-up, screen time

## 3. Unit 1: Sequences & Patterns


### Lesson Plan — “Pattern Play”


**Objective**
Students will recognize, extend, and create simple AB and ABC repeating patterns.

**Materials**
Colored blocks or beads, pattern cards, a simple “what comes next” picture sheet.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Clap a simple pattern (clap-clap-stomp, clap-clap-stomp) and have children join in.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Show a pattern with colored blocks (red-blue-red-blue). Ask children what comes next and why.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children build their own simple pattern with blocks or beads and challenge a partner to extend it.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Show a new pattern; children say what comes next as a group.

**Differentiation**
Support: use simple AB patterns only with high color contrast. Stretch: introduce ABC or ABB patterns for more challenge.

## 4. Unit 2: Unplugged Coding Basics


### Lesson Plan — “Be the Robot!”


**Objective**
Students will give and follow step-by-step instructions to move through a simple unplugged maze, and identify and fix a simple error in a sequence.

**Materials**
Masking tape or chalk to create a simple floor path, direction cards (forward, backward, turn left, turn right), a stuffed animal “robot.”

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Play “Simon Says” using movement directions (forward, backward, turn around).

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Create a simple floor path with tape. One child is the “programmer” and gives step-by-step directions (forward 2 steps, turn right, forward 1 step) to a partner “robot” who follows exactly what is said.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
Introduce a simple “bug” — give a sequence that doesn't quite work (the robot bumps into a wall) and ask the class to fix (“debug”) the instructions together.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What happens if we give the directions in the wrong order?”

**Differentiation**
Support: use only 2-step sequences with big, clear movements. Stretch: introduce a simple “loop” concept (repeat these 2 steps 3 times) to reach a farther goal.

## 5. Unit 3: Meet the Computer


### Lesson Plan — “Meet the Computer”


**Objective**
Students will identify basic parts of a computer and practice basic mouse and keyboard awareness.

**Materials**
A computer, laptop, or tablet (or pictures of one), a simple labeled diagram of computer parts.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Where have you seen a computer before?” (home, store, library)

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Show a computer and point out the screen, keyboard, and mouse or trackpad. Explain simply what each part does.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
If devices are available, practice simple click-and-drag or basic navigation together; otherwise, practice with a labeled paper diagram, pointing to each part when named.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask children to name one place they've seen a computer or device being used.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on naming just the 3 main parts (screen, keyboard, mouse). Stretch: introduce other devices (tablet, phone) and discuss similarities and differences.

## 6. Unit 4: Staying Safe Online


### Lesson Plan — “Staying Safe With Screens”


**Objective**
Students will understand the rule of asking a grown-up before using a device and recognize that personal information should stay private.

**Materials**
Simple scenario cards (e.g., “An app asks for your address”), a “safe or not safe” sorting activity.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What is a rule you follow before using a tablet or computer at home?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Discuss the rule of always asking a grown-up first before using a device or app. Introduce the idea that some information (like your address or full name) should stay private and only be shared with trusted grown-ups.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
Sort simple scenario cards into “ask a grown-up” or “this is fine” categories as a class, discussing each one.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What should you do if something on a screen makes you feel confused or uncomfortable?” (Tell a grown-up right away.)

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the single core rule — always ask a grown-up first. Stretch: role-play a few more nuanced scenarios and discuss the reasoning together.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

Keep this low-stress and playful — most of Kindergarten Technology & Logic is unplugged. This guide covers the full Kindergarten year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Recognizes, extends, and creates simple patterns
- Gives and follows step-by-step instructions and can debug a simple error
- Identifies basic computer parts and their functions
- Understands the ask-a-grown-up-first rule and keeps personal information private

**Question Types to Expect**
Pattern completion, sequencing and ordering, matching and labeling, true/false, and simple explanations.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Turn everyday routines into ‘algorithms’ (the steps to get ready for bed)
- Co-view or co-use any screen time together
- Reinforce the ‘ask first’ rule consistently across all devices
- Praise curiosity about how things work

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
