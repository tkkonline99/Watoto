# STORYBOOKS — GRADE 3 (AGES 8–9)

Reading Level Guide
Master Curriculum Project
As in earlier grades, Storybooks stays a levelled reading library rather than a tested subject — no worksheet, quiz, or exam guide, just reading-level guidance and categories. Use it alongside the Grade 3 Reading & Writing package, especially Unit 3 (Figurative Language) and Unit 4 (Research Skills & Genres).

## Reading Level for This Age

Grade 3 spans confident independent reading — roughly Guided Reading Level M–P (or Lexile 500–700). Books at this level typically feature:
- Full-length chapter books with multiple chapters and few or no illustrations
- More sophisticated plots, including subplots and multiple characters
- Figurative language (similes, metaphors, idioms) woven into the text
- A wider range of genres: realistic fiction, fantasy, historical fiction, mystery, and nonfiction
- Vocabulary that stretches the reader and rewards using context clues

## Book Categories & Anchors


### Chapter Book Series Across Genres

Longer series across different genres build stamina and let readers follow characters they love. Well-known anchors at this level include the “Magic Tree House” series (adventure/history), “The Boxcar Children” (mystery), and “Mercy Watson” for readers still building confidence.

### Levelled Readers (Guided Reading M–P)

Most reading programs and libraries organize these explicitly by guided reading letter — look for M through P labels for on-level Grade 3 texts.

### Genre Sampler

Grade 3 is a great time to deliberately sample genres, directly supporting Reading & Writing Unit 4: try a fantasy, a mystery, a book of poetry, a fable collection, and a nonfiction book across the year.

### Content-Area Nonfiction

- Books about ecosystems, forces, weather, and space (ties into Science Units 1–4)
- Books about government, geography, history, and economics (ties into Social Studies Units 1–4)
- Nonfiction series with text features like headings, captions, and diagrams

### Figurative-Language-Rich Fiction

Books that use vivid similes, metaphors, and descriptive language reinforce Reading & Writing Unit 3, and help readers appreciate how authors 'paint pictures' with words.

## How to Use This List

By Grade 3, independent reading is the main event, with read-alouds reserved for more challenging books and shared enjoyment:
- Independent reading: the child reads chapter books at their level, using context clues and figurative-language awareness from Reading & Writing
- Read-aloud / book club: an adult reads or discusses more advanced books, exploring theme, character motivation, and author's craft
30 minutes or more of daily independent reading is a strong target for this age.

## Light Comprehension Check-Ins

Deepen discussion to include theme, genre, and author's craft:
- “What genre is this, and how can you tell?”
- “Can you find an example of figurative language? What does it mean?”
- “What is the theme or lesson of this story?”
- “How did the main character change from beginning to end?”
