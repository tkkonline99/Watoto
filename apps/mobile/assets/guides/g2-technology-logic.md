## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 2 Technology & Logic year, building on Grade 1's intro to block coding.

### Unit 1: Loops & Repetition in Coding (Weeks 1–9)

- Understand that a loop repeats a set of commands
- Use a loop to make a program shorter and more efficient
- Predict how many times a loop will run
- Identify when to use a loop instead of repeating blocks
Key vocabulary: loop, repeat, efficient, program, command

### Unit 2: Debugging & Problem Solving (Weeks 10–18)

- Find and fix errors (bugs) in a sequence of commands
- Test a program and observe whether it works
- Use logical steps to figure out where a problem is
- Understand that debugging is a normal part of coding
Key vocabulary: bug, debug, test, error, logical

### Unit 3: Search & Digital Tools (Weeks 19–27)

- Understand that computers help us find information
- Use simple, safe search terms to find information (with guidance)
- Recognize that not everything online is true
- Use basic digital tools (a simple drawing or writing program)
Key vocabulary: search, information, keyword, digital tool, reliable

### Unit 4: Safe & Responsible Online (Weeks 28–36)

- Reinforce online safety habits (asking first, keeping info private)
- Understand what kind of information is safe to share and what isn't
- Recognize kind vs. unkind online behavior and how to respond
- Understand the basics of a digital footprint (what we do online can stay online)
Key vocabulary: private, digital footprint, responsible, kind

## 3. Unit 1: Loops & Repetition in Coding


### Lesson Plan — “Loops Make It Easier”


**Objective**
Students will understand loops and use them to make a program shorter.

**Materials**
Block-coding app or unplugged block cards including a ‘repeat’ block, a grid or maze printout.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Have children clap 4 times, then say: ‘What if I said repeat clap 4 times?’ Discuss how that's shorter.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Show a sequence with the same block repeated 4 times, then replace it with a ‘repeat 4 times’ loop. Discuss how loops make programs shorter and easier.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children rewrite a repetitive block sequence using a loop.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “When is it a good idea to use a loop?”

**Differentiation**
Support: use very short sequences (repeat 2–3 times). Stretch: introduce a loop inside a longer sequence with other commands.

## 4. Unit 2: Debugging & Problem Solving


### Lesson Plan — “Finding the Bug”


**Objective**
Students will find and fix errors (bugs) in a sequence of commands.

**Materials**
Block-coding app or unplugged cards, a few pre-made 'buggy' sequences, a simple maze.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a sequence where a character bumps into a wall; ask: ‘What went wrong?’

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the word 'bug' (an error) and 'debug' (finding and fixing it). Model checking a sequence step by step to find where it goes wrong, then fixing it.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children fix a 'buggy' sequence so the character reaches its goal.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How did you figure out where the problem was?”

**Differentiation**
Support: give a sequence with one obvious error. Stretch: give a sequence with two errors or a subtler mistake.

### Practice Worksheet

## 5. Unit 3: Search & Digital Tools


### Lesson Plan — “Finding Information Safely”


**Objective**
Students will understand that computers help us find information and that not everything online is true.

**Materials**
A teacher-controlled device or printed examples of search results, a simple drawing or writing program if available.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “If you wanted to learn about lions, how could a computer help you?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Discuss how search engines help us find information using keywords. Emphasize using simple, safe search terms with a grown-up. Explain that not everything online is true, so we check with trusted sources.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, brainstorm good keywords for a simple question and discuss what makes a source trustworthy.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How can you tell if information might not be true?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the idea of keywords and asking a grown-up. Stretch: compare two 'sources' and discuss which is more reliable and why.

## 6. Unit 4: Safe & Responsible Online


### Lesson Plan — “Our Digital Footprint”


**Objective**
Students will reinforce online safety and understand that what we do online can stay online (a digital footprint).

**Materials**
Scenario cards (online situations), a simple 'safe to share / keep private' sorting activity.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Review: “What information should we keep private online?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce 'digital footprint' — the idea that things we post or do online can stay there. Discuss what's safe to share (a nice comment, a drawing you made) vs. private (your address, password, full name). Reinforce kind online behavior.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
Sort scenario cards into 'safe to share' and 'keep private' as a class.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why should we think before we post something online?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on safe-to-share vs. private. Stretch: discuss how a digital footprint could affect you later and role-play responding to unkindness online.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 2 Technology & Logic year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Uses loops to make programs shorter and predicts loop behavior
- Finds and fixes bugs in a sequence and tests programs
- Understands searching for information and that not everything online is true
- Follows safe, responsible, and kind online habits and understands digital footprints

**Question Types to Expect**
Loop and sequence questions, debugging scenarios, search and reliability questions, true/false, and short explanations.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Try free coding games that introduce loops together
- Frame everyday problem-solving as 'debugging'
- Search for kid-friendly information together and talk about reliable sources
- Reinforce privacy, kindness, and thinking before posting online

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
