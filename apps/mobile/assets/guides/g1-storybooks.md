# STORYBOOKS — GRADE 1 (AGES 6–7)

Reading Level Guide
Master Curriculum Project
As in Kindergarten, Storybooks stays a levelled reading library rather than a tested subject — no worksheet, quiz, or exam guide, just reading-level guidance and categories. Use it alongside the Grade 1 Reading & Writing package, especially Unit 2 (Fluency & Sight Words) and Unit 4 (Reading for Meaning).

## Reading Level for This Age

Grade 1 spans early-to-mid emergent reading — roughly Guided Reading Level D–J (or Lexile 100–400). Books at this level typically feature:
- Longer sentences with more varied structure, less repetitive than Kindergarten texts
- A mix of decodable words and an expanding sight-word vocabulary
- Simple chapter structure beginning to appear (short chapters, 2–4 pages each)
- Clear story arcs with a beginning, problem, and resolution
- Illustrations that support but no longer fully carry the meaning

## Book Categories & Anchors


### Early Chapter Book Series

Short, illustrated chapter books written in series, so children can build confidence and familiarity with recurring characters. Widely used anchors include the “Frog and Toad” books by Arnold Lobel and early “Magic Tree House” titles by Mary Pope Osborne.

### Levelled Readers (Guided Reading D–J)

Most reading programs and libraries organize these explicitly by guided reading letter or a similar level — look for stickers or catalog labels marking the range.

### Rhyming & Wordplay Books

Continue building phonological awareness and reading enjoyment with playful, rhythmic texts — further Dr. Seuss titles are a well-known anchor here.

### Concept & Nonfiction Books That Reinforce Other Subjects

- Simple nonfiction about life cycles or earth materials (ties into Science Units 1–2)
- Books about community helpers, maps, or the past (ties into Social Studies Units 1, 3, and 4)
- Beginner nonfiction series designed for early readers, often labeled ‘Level 1’ readers

### Fiction vs. Nonfiction Pairs

Deliberately pair a fiction and nonfiction book on the same topic (e.g., a story about a dog and a nonfiction book about dogs) to directly support Reading & Writing Unit 4's fiction/nonfiction skill.

## How to Use This List

Continue the read-aloud and shared/independent reading split from Kindergarten, gradually shifting more time to independent reading as fluency grows:
- Read-aloud time: an adult reads a chapter book slightly above the child's independent level
- Independent time: the child reads a levelled book on their own, sounding out unfamiliar words using digraph and blend skills from Reading & Writing Unit 1
20–25 minutes of daily reading time, split between the two modes, is a strong target for this age.

## Light Comprehension Check-Ins

Building on Kindergarten retelling skills, add a few slightly deeper questions:
- “What was the main idea of this chapter?”
- “Was this book fiction or nonfiction? How do you know?”
- “What do you think will happen next?”
- “Has anything like this ever happened to you?”
