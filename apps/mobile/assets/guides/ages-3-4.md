# AGES 3–4 — PRESCHOOL

Preschool Learning Guide
Master Curriculum Project — Early Years

## About This Guide

This is a dedicated, deeper companion to the combined Early Years guide, focused on the younger preschool year. It is organized by the same six subjects the app uses. Preschoolers learn through play, conversation, imagination, and hands-on exploration — still not through worksheets or tests. This is a wonderful age of ‘why?’ questions, elaborate pretend play, and the first real interest in letters and numbers.

## Developmental Snapshot (Ages 3–4)

Every child develops at their own pace — these are general patterns, not a checklist to rush.

**Body & movement**
- Pedals a tricycle, hops, and throws and catches a ball
- Uses child-safe scissors and draws simple shapes like a circle
- Dresses with some help and builds tall block towers

**Communication & language**
- Speaks in longer sentences and tells simple stories
- Asks lots of ‘why?’ questions and is understood by most people
- Knows and may name some letters, and enjoys rhymes

**Social & emotional**
- Begins cooperative play and starts making friends
- Takes turns with support and follows simple game rules
- Shows more empathy and engages in rich, imaginative play

**Thinking & learning**
- Counts to ten or beyond and knows some colors and shapes
- Sorts by more than one feature and completes bigger puzzles
- Understands ideas like day and night and sequences familiar events

## 1. Early Language & Literacy

This is the app’s ‘Reading & Writing’ subject. Preschoolers build rich vocabulary and begin to notice that print carries meaning. Rhyme, letter play, and storytelling prepare them to read.

**Learning goals**
- Enjoys rhymes and begins to hear the sounds in words
- Recognizes and names some letters
- Attempts to write their name and ‘tells’ stories through drawing

**Play-based activities**
- Play rhyming games (‘cat, hat, … bat!’) and sing the alphabet
- Go on a letter hunt for the first letter of your child’s name
- Ask ‘what sound does ‘ball’ start with?’ to build sound awareness
- Retell favorite stories together and talk about what happens
- Encourage your child to draw a picture and tell you about it (you can write their words down)
- Point out print in the world — signs, labels, menus — to show that letters mean things

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child joining in with rhymes, recognizing letters (especially in their name), and using drawing to tell stories. These are early reading and writing in action.

## 2. Early Numeracy & Thinking

This is the app’s ‘Math’ subject. Preschoolers count with growing accuracy, recognize numerals, and explore patterns and shapes — all through games and everyday moments.

**Learning goals**
- Counts objects with growing one-to-one accuracy
- Recognizes some written numerals
- Creates and continues simple patterns

**Play-based activities**
- Count real objects by touching each one (‘one… two… three cookies’)
- Make simple patterns with blocks or beads (red, blue, red, blue…)
- Match numerals to the right number of objects
- Sort a collection by two features (‘big red ones’ vs. ‘small blue ones’)
- Compare groups — ‘which plate has more?’
- Go on a shape hunt around the house (‘find something round!’)

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child counting accurately, spotting patterns, and using number and shape words. Understanding matters far more than memorizing at this age.

## 3. Exploring the World

This is the app’s ‘Science’ subject. Preschoolers investigate, predict, and ask endless questions. Simple experiments and nature study feed their curiosity.

**Learning goals**
- Asks questions and makes simple predictions
- Observes changes in nature and materials
- Explores cause and effect through simple experiments

**Play-based activities**
- Plant seeds and observe them grow, drawing what you see over time
- Keep a simple weather chart and talk about the seasons
- Explore sink and float with different objects, guessing first
- Use a magnifying glass to look closely at leaves, bugs, and rocks
- Mix colors with paint or colored water and predict what will happen
- Talk about life cycles (a seed to a plant, a caterpillar to a butterfly)

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child asking questions, making guesses, and observing closely. Encouraging their ‘why?’ is the best science lesson at this age.

## 4. Me & My World

This is the app’s ‘Social Studies’ subject. Preschoolers learn about feelings, friendship, family, and community, and begin to understand fairness and taking turns.

**Learning goals**
- Names and begins to manage feelings
- Plays cooperatively and takes turns with support
- Understands family roles and community helpers

**Play-based activities**
- Sort or name feelings using faces (‘happy, sad, angry, excited’)
- Set up dramatic play about community helpers (doctor, chef, mail carrier)
- Talk about family traditions and look at photos together
- Draw a simple map of your child’s bedroom or a familiar route
- Play turn-taking games to practice patience and fairness
- Read stories about friendship, kindness, and different families and cultures

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child naming feelings, taking turns, and showing kindness. These social-emotional skills are among the most important learning of the preschool years.

## 5. Cause, Effect & Problem-Solving

This is the app’s ‘Technology & Logic’ subject. At this age it means sequencing, sorting, patterns, and ‘unplugged’ logic games — the thinking skills behind coding, without needing a screen.

**Learning goals**
- Puts events in order (first, next, last)
- Sorts and classifies objects by rules
- Solves simple puzzles and building challenges

**Play-based activities**
- Play ‘unplugged coding’: give step-by-step directions to move to a toy (‘two steps forward, turn, one step’)
- Use sequencing cards or photos to order a routine (first, next, last)
- Build patterns and structures with blocks and talk about how they work
- Do bigger jigsaw puzzles together
- Sort a mixed basket of objects by a rule your child chooses
- Introduce simple board games that involve following rules and taking turns

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child ordering steps, following rules, and persisting at puzzles. This step-by-step thinking is the foundation of logic and coding.

## 6. Books & Stories

This is the app’s ‘Storybooks’ subject. Preschoolers can follow longer stories and begin to understand how stories work — with a beginning, middle, and end.

**Learning goals**
- Follows a longer story and recalls parts of it
- Understands that stories have a beginning, middle, and end
- Predicts what might happen next

**Play-based activities**
- Read longer picture books and talk about the beginning, middle, and end
- Pause and ask ‘what do you think happens next?’
- Retell a favorite story together in order
- Act out or use puppets to bring stories to life
- Explore favorite authors and let your child choose books
- Connect stories to your child’s own experiences (‘Remember when we…?’)

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child predicting, retelling, and connecting stories to their life. This comprehension is the heart of becoming a reader.

## Everyday Routines & Caregiver Tips

Preschoolers thrive on rich conversation, playful learning, and consistent, loving limits.
- Answer ‘why?’ questions patiently — curiosity is how preschoolers learn
- Build learning into daily life: counting stairs, sorting laundry, naming letters on signs
- Give your child real responsibilities (setting napkins, watering a plant) to build confidence
- Coach feelings and problem-solving gently rather than solving every conflict for them
- Protect plenty of unstructured, imaginative play — it is serious learning at this age

## Safety & Wellbeing

Preschoolers are more capable but still need supervision and clear limits.
- Supervise near water, streets, and playground equipment
- Teach simple safety rules (holding hands to cross, asking a grown-up first)
- Keep medicines, cleaning products, and small hazards locked away
- Balance screen time with active play, outdoor time, and enough sleep
- Encourage healthy habits — varied foods, hand-washing, and a calm bedtime routine

## When to Check with a Professional

Children this age vary widely, and a single ‘late’ skill is usually nothing to worry about. Still, trust your instincts and talk with your pediatrician if, over time, your child: is very hard for others to understand; does not speak in sentences; shows little interest in playing with others or in pretend play; cannot follow simple two-step instructions; or seems to lose skills. Early support, when needed, makes a real difference — and asking is always okay.
