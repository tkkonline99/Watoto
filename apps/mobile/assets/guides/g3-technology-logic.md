## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 3 Technology & Logic year, building on Grade 2's loops and debugging.

### Unit 1: Conditionals in Coding (Weeks 1–9)

- Understand that conditionals make decisions (if/then/else)
- Use a conditional to make a program respond to different situations
- Combine conditionals with loops
- Predict how a program with a conditional will behave
Key vocabulary: conditional, if/then/else, decision, condition

### Unit 2: Computational Thinking (Weeks 10–18)

- Break complex problems into smaller parts (decomposition)
- Recognize patterns to solve problems
- Focus on important details and ignore the rest (abstraction)
- Create step-by-step solutions (algorithms)
Key vocabulary: decomposition, pattern, abstraction, algorithm

### Unit 3: How Computers Work (Weeks 19–27)

- Identify basic computer parts and their functions (input, output, storage, processor)
- Understand that computers follow programs (instructions)
- Distinguish between hardware and software
- Understand that computers store and process information as data
Key vocabulary: hardware, software, input, output, data

### Unit 4: Digital Citizenship & Privacy (Weeks 28–36)

- Understand digital citizenship (being safe, kind, and responsible online)
- Protect personal information and understand privacy
- Recognize and respond to cyberbullying appropriately
- Evaluate whether online information is reliable
Key vocabulary: digital citizen, privacy, cyberbullying, reliable

## 3. Unit 1: Conditionals in Coding


### Lesson Plan — “Making Decisions with Code”


**Objective**
Students will understand conditionals (if/then/else) and use them to make a program respond to situations.

**Materials**
Block-coding app or unplugged cards including 'if/then' blocks, scenario cards.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Say: 'If it's raining, then bring an umbrella.' Ask for other 'if/then' examples from daily life.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce conditionals: 'if (condition) then (action).' Show how a program can do different things based on a condition (if touching wall, then turn). Introduce if/then/else.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children write a simple conditional for a scenario (if character reaches goal, then celebrate).

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How does a conditional make a program smarter?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on simple if/then. Stretch: combine a conditional inside a loop.

## 4. Unit 2: Computational Thinking


### Lesson Plan — “Thinking Like a Computer Scientist”


**Objective**
Students will break problems into parts, recognize patterns, and create algorithms.

**Materials**
Problem-solving task cards, pattern examples, an algorithm-writing template.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a big task (planning a party); ask how to break it into smaller jobs.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the four parts of computational thinking: decomposition (break it down), pattern recognition (find repeats), abstraction (focus on what matters), and algorithms (step-by-step solution). Work through an example.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children decompose a task and write a simple algorithm for it.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How does breaking a problem into parts make it easier?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on decomposition and algorithms. Stretch: apply all four concepts to one problem.

## 5. Unit 3: How Computers Work


### Lesson Plan — “Inside a Computer”


**Objective**
Students will identify basic computer parts and distinguish hardware from software.

**Materials**
Diagram of computer parts, examples of hardware and software, input/output examples.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What parts does a computer have, and what does each do?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce input (keyboard, mouse), output (screen, speakers), storage (hard drive), and processor (the 'brain'). Distinguish hardware (physical parts) from software (programs). Explain that computers process information as data.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children sort examples into hardware/software and input/output.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What's the difference between hardware and software?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on input/output and hardware/software. Stretch: discuss how data is stored and processed.

## 6. Unit 4: Digital Citizenship & Privacy


### Lesson Plan — “Being a Great Digital Citizen”


**Objective**
Students will understand digital citizenship, protect personal information, and respond to cyberbullying.

**Materials**
Scenario cards (online situations), a privacy checklist, reliability examples.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What does it mean to be a good citizen online, not just in person?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Discuss digital citizenship: being safe, kind, and responsible. Review protecting personal information (privacy). Discuss what cyberbullying is and how to respond (don't engage, save evidence, tell a trusted adult). Introduce checking if online information is reliable.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children respond to scenario cards about online situations.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What should you do if you see cyberbullying?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on privacy and kindness. Stretch: discuss evaluating source reliability in more depth.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 3 Technology & Logic year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Uses conditionals (if/then/else) in programs and predicts behavior
- Applies computational thinking (decomposition, patterns, abstraction, algorithms)
- Identifies computer parts and distinguishes hardware from software
- Practices digital citizenship, protects privacy, and evaluates reliability

**Question Types to Expect**
Conditional logic, computational thinking application, hardware/software and input/output sorting, and digital citizenship scenarios.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Explore coding platforms that use conditionals together
- Frame everyday problem-solving with computational thinking language
- Point out inputs and outputs on devices at home
- Have ongoing conversations about privacy, kindness, and reliable sources online

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
