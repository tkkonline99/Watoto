Complete Curriculum Package — All 4 Units
Master Curriculum Project

## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Science |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 7–8 (Grade 2) |

| Learning Approach | More structured investigations with predictions, observations, and simple conclusions; builds on Grade 1 life cycles and materials |

## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 2 Science year.

### Unit 1: Habitats & Adaptation (Weeks 1–9)

- Describe different habitats and the living things found in them
- Explain how animals and plants are suited to their habitats (adaptation)
- Understand that living things depend on their environment
- Compare how different habitats meet living things' needs
Key vocabulary: habitat, adaptation, environment, survive, depend

### Unit 2: States of Matter (Weeks 10–18)

- Identify solids, liquids, and gases and their properties
- Describe how matter can change state (melting, freezing)
- Sort materials by state of matter
- Observe and record changes in matter
Key vocabulary: solid, liquid, gas, matter, melt, freeze

### Unit 3: Earth's Surface & Landforms (Weeks 19–27)

- Identify common landforms (mountain, valley, river, lake, plain)
- Understand that Earth's surface can change slowly or quickly
- Describe how water shapes the land
- Recognize maps as a way to show landforms
Key vocabulary: landform, mountain, valley, erosion, surface

### Unit 4: Simple Engineering & Design (Weeks 28–36)

- Understand the basic engineering design process (ask, imagine, plan, create, improve)
- Identify a simple problem and brainstorm solutions
- Build and test a simple structure or solution
- Improve a design based on testing
Key vocabulary: engineer, design, problem, solution, improve

## 3. Unit 1: Habitats & Adaptation


### Lesson Plan — “Built for Their Home”


**Objective**
Students will describe habitats and explain how living things are adapted to survive in them.

**Materials**
Habitat picture cards (desert, ocean, forest, arctic), animal picture cards, matching mats.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why do you think a polar bear has thick fur? Where does it live?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce several habitats and discuss features of each. Show how specific animals are adapted (camel stores fat for the desert; fish have gills for water).

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children match animals to their habitats and explain one adaptation.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask children to name one animal and one way it's suited to its home.

**Differentiation**
Support: use very clear animal-habitat pairs. Stretch: discuss what might happen if an animal were moved to a different habitat.

## 4. Unit 2: States of Matter


### Lesson Plan — “Solid, Liquid, Gas”


**Objective**
Students will identify solids, liquids, and gases and describe how matter changes state.

**Materials**
Examples of solids (block, rock), a cup of water, a balloon (for gas), an ice cube for melting.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Is water always a liquid? What happens when it gets very cold or very hot?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the three states with examples. Discuss properties (solids hold shape, liquids flow and take the container's shape, gases spread out). Watch an ice cube begin to melt.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children sort picture cards or classroom objects into solid, liquid, gas.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What happens to ice when it warms up? What is that change called?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on solids vs. liquids first. Stretch: introduce freezing and melting as reversible changes, and discuss evaporation.

## 5. Unit 3: Earth's Surface & Landforms


### Lesson Plan — “Shaping the Land”


**Objective**
Students will identify common landforms and understand that Earth's surface changes over time.

**Materials**
Landform picture cards (mountain, valley, river, lake, plain), a shallow tray with sand and water for erosion demo.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What are some different kinds of land you've seen — mountains, rivers, flat fields?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce landforms with pictures. Demonstrate erosion by pouring a little water over a small sand hill in a tray, showing how water moves and shapes land.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children match landform names to pictures and describe each.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Can land change quickly (like in a flood) or slowly (like a river carving a canyon)?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on 3 very distinct landforms. Stretch: discuss how both slow (erosion) and fast (volcano, landslide) changes shape Earth's surface.

## 6. Unit 4: Simple Engineering & Design


### Lesson Plan — “Think Like an Engineer”


**Objective**
Students will use the engineering design process to build and improve a simple structure.

**Materials**
Building materials (paper, tape, straws, blocks, or craft sticks), a simple challenge prompt (e.g., ‘build a tower that stands on its own’).

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What does an engineer do? Have you ever built something and had to fix it?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the design process: Ask (what's the problem?), Imagine (brainstorm), Plan (sketch), Create (build), Improve (test and fix). Model with a simple example.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children plan and build a simple structure to meet a challenge, then test it.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet (reflect on their design).

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What would you change to make your design better?”

**Differentiation**
Support: provide a simpler challenge and pre-cut materials. Stretch: add a constraint (must be a certain height, or hold a small weight).

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 2 Science year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Describes habitats and how living things are adapted to them
- Identifies solids, liquids, and gases and how matter changes state
- Identifies landforms and understands Earth's surface changes over time
- Uses the engineering design process to build and improve a solution

**Question Types to Expect**
Matching, sorting, fill-in-the-blank, true/false, sequencing (design process), and short explanations with drawings.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Visit different habitats (park, pond, forest) and talk about the animals there
- Freeze and melt water at home to explore states of matter
- Look at landforms on trips or in photos and name them
- Build things together and talk through the design process

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
