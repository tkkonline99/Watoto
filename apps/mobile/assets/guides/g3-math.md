## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Math |
|---|---|

## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 3 Math year.

### Unit 1: Multiplication & Division Facts (Weeks 1–9)

- Understand division as sharing equally or making equal groups
- Fluently multiply and divide within 100
- Understand the relationship between multiplication and division (fact families)
- Solve word problems using multiplication and division
Key vocabulary: multiply, divide, product, quotient, fact family

### Unit 2: Fractions as Numbers (Weeks 10–18)

- Understand a fraction as a number on the number line
- Recognize and generate equivalent fractions
- Compare fractions with the same numerator or denominator
- Represent fractions using models and number lines
Key vocabulary: fraction, numerator, denominator, equivalent, number line

### Unit 3: Area & Perimeter (Weeks 19–27)

- Understand area as the amount of space inside a shape, measured in square units
- Calculate the area of rectangles by multiplying side lengths
- Understand perimeter as the distance around a shape
- Solve problems involving area and perimeter
Key vocabulary: area, perimeter, square unit, length, width

### Unit 4: Multi-Digit Addition & Subtraction (Weeks 28–36)

- Add and subtract within 1000 fluently, using regrouping
- Round numbers to the nearest 10 or 100
- Estimate sums and differences
- Solve multi-step word problems
Key vocabulary: regroup, round, estimate, multi-step

## 3. Unit 1: Multiplication & Division Facts


### Lesson Plan — “Multiplication and Division Together”


**Objective**
Students will fluently multiply and divide within 100 and understand their relationship.

**Materials**
Multiplication chart, counters, fact family triangles.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Skip count by a chosen number; connect it to a multiplication fact.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Model division as the inverse of multiplication (if 3×4=12, then 12÷4=3). Use fact family triangles to show the connection.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children complete fact families and practice a set of facts.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How can knowing 6×7 help you solve 42÷6?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the 2, 5, and 10 fact families. Stretch: introduce trickier facts (7, 8) and two-step problems.

## 4. Unit 2: Fractions as Numbers


### Lesson Plan — “Fractions on the Number Line”


**Objective**
Students will understand fractions as numbers and recognize equivalent fractions.

**Materials**
Fraction strips, number lines, fraction circles.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Fold a strip of paper into equal parts; label each part as a fraction.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Place fractions on a number line (0 to 1). Show equivalent fractions using fraction strips (1/2 = 2/4). Compare fractions with the same denominator or numerator.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children build equivalent fractions with strips and place fractions on a number line.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How do you know 1/2 and 2/4 are equal?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on halves and fourths with visual models. Stretch: introduce comparing fractions with different denominators using models.

## 5. Unit 3: Area & Perimeter


### Lesson Plan — “Measuring Space and Distance”


**Objective**
Students will calculate the area and perimeter of rectangles.

**Materials**
Grid paper, rulers, square tiles.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Count the squares inside a shape drawn on grid paper.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce area (space inside, in square units) — for a rectangle, length × width. Introduce perimeter (distance around) — add all sides. Work an example of each.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children find area and perimeter of a few rectangles on grid paper.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What's the difference between area and perimeter?”

**Differentiation**
Support: use grid paper to count squares for area. Stretch: find a missing side length given area or perimeter.

## 6. Unit 4: Multi-Digit Addition & Subtraction


### Lesson Plan — “Big Numbers, Rounding, and Estimating”


**Objective**
Students will add and subtract within 1000 and round numbers to estimate.

**Materials**
Base-ten blocks, place value chart, whiteboard.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Round a few numbers to the nearest 10 together (47 → 50).

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Model adding and subtracting three-digit numbers with regrouping. Show how rounding first lets you estimate an answer, then check the exact answer against it.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children solve a few three-digit problems and estimate first.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why is estimating useful before you calculate?”

**Differentiation**
Support: keep to problems without regrouping first. Stretch: introduce multi-step word problems combining operations.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 3 Math year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Multiplies and divides fluently within 100 and understands their relationship
- Understands fractions as numbers and recognizes equivalent fractions
- Calculates area and perimeter of rectangles
- Adds and subtracts within 1000 and rounds to estimate

**Question Types to Expect**
Multiplication/division facts and word problems, fraction identification and comparison, area/perimeter calculation, and multi-digit computation with estimation.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Practice multiplication facts in short daily sessions
- Use measuring cups and recipes to explore fractions
- Measure rooms or objects to practice area and perimeter
- Estimate costs while shopping together

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
