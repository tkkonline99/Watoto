## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Reading & Writing |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 9–10 (Grade 4) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Structured essay writing, close reading, and vocabulary in context prepare students for the jump to upper elementary and middle grades |

## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 4 Reading & Writing year, the final band in this curriculum.

### Unit 1: Essay Structure (Narrative, Persuasive, Informational) (Weeks 1–9)

- Write a structured essay with a clear thesis, body paragraphs, and conclusion
- Distinguish and write narrative, persuasive, and informational pieces
- Support ideas with reasons, details, and examples
- Use transitions to connect paragraphs smoothly
Key vocabulary: essay, thesis, narrative, persuasive, informational

### Unit 2: Vocabulary in Context (Weeks 10–18)

- Determine the meaning of unfamiliar words using context clues
- Understand and use prefixes, suffixes, and root words
- Distinguish shades of meaning among related words
- Use new vocabulary accurately in writing
Key vocabulary: context clue, prefix, suffix, root word, synonym

### Unit 3: Close Reading & Analysis (Weeks 19–27)

- Analyze a text's main idea and how details support it
- Identify theme and summarize a text accurately
- Compare and contrast texts, characters, or points of view
- Cite specific evidence from the text to support answers
Key vocabulary: theme, summary, analyze, point of view, evidence

### Unit 4: Grammar Mastery (Weeks 28–36)

- Use correct punctuation, including commas, quotation marks, and apostrophes
- Identify and fix run-on sentences and fragments
- Use a variety of sentence structures (simple, compound, complex)
- Apply consistent verb tense and correct subject-verb agreement
Key vocabulary: run-on, fragment, compound, complex, punctuation

## 3. Unit 1: Essay Structure


### Lesson Plan — “Writing a Structured Essay”


**Objective**
Students will write a structured essay with a thesis, body paragraphs, and conclusion, and distinguish essay types.

**Materials**
Essay planning organizer, sample essays of each type, lined paper.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show three short essay openings; ask which is telling a story, which is arguing a point, and which is giving facts.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the three essay types (narrative, persuasive, informational) and the structure: introduction with a thesis, body paragraphs with support, and a conclusion. Model planning one with an organizer.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, plan an essay on a shared topic using the organizer.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet (plan and begin an essay).

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Children share their thesis statement with a partner.

**Differentiation**
Support: provide a fill-in essay frame. Stretch: require three body paragraphs with distinct supporting points.

## 4. Unit 2: Vocabulary in Context


### Lesson Plan — “Unlocking Word Meanings”


**Objective**
Students will use context clues and word parts (prefixes, suffixes, roots) to determine word meanings.

**Materials**
Passages with challenging vocabulary, a prefix/suffix/root reference chart.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a sentence with a made-up word; ask students to guess its meaning from context.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Model using context clues to figure out an unfamiliar word. Then introduce common prefixes (un-, re-, pre-), suffixes (-ful, -less, -ly), and roots, showing how they signal meaning.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children use context and word parts to define unfamiliar words in a passage.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Children share one new word they figured out and how.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on context clues with clear supporting sentences. Stretch: build new words using multiple prefixes/suffixes and roots.

## 5. Unit 3: Close Reading & Analysis


### Lesson Plan — “Reading Closely for Meaning”


**Objective**
Students will analyze a text's main idea and theme, and cite evidence to support answers.

**Materials**
A short rich text (story or article), highlighters, a text-evidence response template.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What's the difference between what a story is about and what it's really about (its theme)?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Model close reading: read once for gist, then again to note main idea, key details, and theme. Show how to cite specific evidence ('In paragraph 2, it says...') to support an answer.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children find text evidence to answer a question about a shared passage.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Children share one piece of text evidence and what it supports.

**Differentiation**
Support: provide sentence starters for citing evidence. Stretch: compare the theme or point of view across two texts.

### Practice Worksheet

## 6. Unit 4: Grammar Mastery


### Lesson Plan — “Polishing Our Grammar”


**Objective**
Students will use correct punctuation and a variety of sentence structures, and fix run-ons and fragments.

**Materials**
Sentence correction worksheets, punctuation reference chart, sentence-combining cards.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a run-on sentence; ask how to fix it.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Review punctuation (commas, quotation marks, apostrophes). Introduce fixing run-ons (too many ideas jammed together) and fragments (incomplete sentences). Show simple, compound, and complex sentence structures.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children fix a set of run-ons and fragments and combine short sentences.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Children share one sentence they improved.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on run-ons/fragments and end punctuation. Stretch: have students write one of each sentence type (simple, compound, complex).

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 4 Reading & Writing year (all 4 units) — the final band in the curriculum.

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Writes structured narrative, persuasive, and informational essays
- Uses context clues and word parts to determine word meanings
- Reads closely, identifies theme, and cites text evidence
- Uses correct punctuation and varied sentence structures, fixing run-ons and fragments

**Question Types to Expect**
Essay writing and planning, vocabulary and word-part questions, close-reading analysis with text evidence, and grammar correction.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Encourage essay writing on topics they care about
- Discuss new words and their parts when reading together
- Ask 'how do you know?' and look for text evidence together
- Proofread writing together, focusing on run-ons, fragments, and punctuation

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
