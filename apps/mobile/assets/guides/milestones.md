# EARLY YEARS MILESTONES & ACTIVITIES GUIDE

Ages 0–5 — Babies, Toddlers & Preschool · All 6 Subjects
Master Curriculum Project
This guide covers the four youngest age bands across all six subjects. At this stage, learning is observational and play-based rather than tested: each entry below lists developmental milestones to watch for and simple activities to support them — there are no worksheets, quizzes, or exams in this guide. Formal tests and quizzes begin at Kindergarten (Ages 5–6), covered in the separate subject packages.

## Ages 0–1 (Babies & Toddlers)


### Reading & Writing


**Milestones**
- Turns toward voices and sounds
- Enjoys being read to and looking at pictures
- Tries to grab or pat board books
- Babbles and makes varied sounds
- Responds to own name

**Activities**
- Read aloud daily with an expressive voice using simple board books
- Sing nursery rhymes and songs together
- Narrate daily routines out loud (“Now we're putting on socks”)
- Offer high-contrast picture books for visual tracking

### Math


**Milestones**
- Explores objects by mouthing, shaking, or dropping them
- Shows interest in size differences (big vs. small objects)
- Begins to anticipate routines (early cause and effect)
- Tracks moving objects with their eyes

**Activities**
- Nesting cups and stacking rings play
- Simple peekaboo and hide-and-seek games
- Sort large, safe objects by size together
- Counting songs with motions (e.g., “This Little Piggy”)

### Science


**Milestones**
- Explores textures and materials with hands and mouth
- Shows curiosity about surroundings
- Reacts to light, sound, and movement
- Begins supervised water or sand exploration

**Activities**
- Sensory bins with safe, textured objects
- Supervised outdoor time noticing wind, leaves, and weather
- Simple “what do you hear/see” narration walks
- Water play at bath time

### Social Studies


**Milestones**
- Recognizes primary caregivers' faces and voices
- Shows attachment and seeks comfort from familiar adults
- Begins to notice other babies and children
- Responds to simple social cues like waving

**Activities**
- Face-to-face interaction and mirror play
- Simple family photo books
- Model waving “hello” and “bye-bye”
- Keep consistent daily routines to build a sense of security

### Technology & Logic


**Milestones**
- Explores cause-and-effect toys (press a button → sound or light)
- Shows early problem-solving (reaching around an obstacle)
- Begins to understand object permanence

**Activities**
- Cause-and-effect toys (activity cubes, pop-up toys)
- Object permanence games (hide a toy under a cloth)
- Simple stacking and nesting toys

### Storybooks


**Recommended**
Board books with high-contrast images, textures (touch-and-feel), and simple rhymes. Several short 5–10 minute shared-reading moments spread through the day work better than one long session at this age.

## Ages 2–3 (Babies & Toddlers)


### Reading & Writing


**Milestones**
- Points to and names familiar pictures
- Enjoys repetitive or rhyming books
- “Pretend reads” by turning pages and talking
- Vocabulary grows rapidly, from ~50 words to short phrases
- Begins to hold a crayon

**Activities**
- Re-read favorite books often — repetition builds language
- Ask “what's this?” while pointing at pictures together
- Scribble with crayons on large paper
- Play simple rhyming games

### Math


**Milestones**
- Counts by rote to 5–10 (may skip numbers)
- Sorts objects by one attribute (color or shape)
- Understands “more” and “big/little”
- Matches identical objects

**Activities**
- Count steps, snacks, or toys together
- Sort laundry or blocks by color
- Play with simple shape-sorter toys
- Compare “more or less” at snack time

### Science


**Milestones**
- Names familiar animals and objects
- Notices weather changes (rainy, sunny)
- Explores materials through play (sand, water, playdough)
- Asks simple “why” questions

**Activities**
- Nature walks naming things you see
- Keep a simple weather chart (“Is it sunny today?”)
- Playdough or sand exploration
- Grow a simple plant together and check on it

### Social Studies


**Milestones**
- Identifies family members by name or relationship
- Begins parallel play with other children
- Expresses basic emotions and starts to name them
- Follows simple family routines

**Activities**
- Family photo naming games
- Name emotions using picture cards or a mirror
- Playdates with turn-taking practice
- Talk through daily routines together

### Technology & Logic


**Milestones**
- Completes simple 2–4 piece puzzles
- Sorts and matches objects by shape or color
- Imitates simple sequences (e.g., claps in a pattern)
- Begins pretend play with cause-effect toys

**Activities**
- Simple chunky puzzles
- Matching games with picture pairs
- Copy-a-pattern clapping games
- Shape sorters and stacking toys

### Storybooks


**Recommended**
Picture books with repetitive, predictable text; lift-the-flap and touch-and-feel books; simple rhyming stories that invite the child to fill in a repeated line.

## Ages 3–4 (Preschool)


### Reading & Writing


**Milestones**
- Recognizes some letters, especially in own name
- Enjoys longer stories with simple plots
- Retells a familiar story with prompting
- Begins rhyming awareness
- Attempts to write or scribble “letters”

**Activities**
- Point out letters in the child's name around the house
- Read aloud and pause to ask “what do you think happens next?”
- Play rhyming games (“what rhymes with cat?”)
- Trace letters in sand or with fingers

### Math


**Milestones**
- Counts to 10–20 with increasing accuracy
- Recognizes and names some numerals
- Shows one-to-one correspondence with small sets
- Identifies basic shapes (circle, square, triangle)
- Compares sizes (bigger/smaller, longer/shorter)

**Activities**
- Count toys or snacks aloud together
- Go on a shape hunt around the house
- Play simple board games with counting spaces
- Compare the height or size of household objects

### Science


**Milestones**
- Names the five senses
- Distinguishes living vs. non-living things with support
- Identifies basic plant and animal parts
- Participates in simple experiments with guidance

**Activities**
- Take a “five senses” walk (what do you see/hear/smell/feel?)
- Try simple sink-or-float experiments in the bath or sink
- Plant seeds and observe their growth
- Sort toys into “living” and “not living” groups

### Social Studies


**Milestones**
- Names community helpers (doctor, firefighter, teacher)
- Understands basic family structure and roles
- Begins to share and take turns with guidance
- Shows awareness of simple rules

**Activities**
- Community-helper dress-up or pretend play
- Talk about family members' roles and jobs
- Practice turn-taking games
- Read books about different families and communities

### Technology & Logic


**Milestones**
- Follows 2-step directions
- Completes simple pattern sequences (red-blue-red-blue, ___)
- Sorts objects by more than one attribute
- Begins simple “if this, then that” reasoning in play

**Activities**
- Build simple patterns with blocks or beads
- Play “Simon Says” style multi-step direction games
- Try unplugged sequencing games (order the steps of getting dressed)
- Co-view any screen time with discussion — digital literacy starts with adult involvement

### Storybooks


**Recommended**
Picture books with simple plots and relatable characters; interactive read-aloud favorites; early alphabet and counting books that tie naturally into Reading & Writing and Math milestones.

## Ages 4–5 (Preschool)


### Reading & Writing


**Milestones**
- Recognizes most or all uppercase letters and many lowercase letters
- Knows the sounds for many letters
- Begins blending simple sounds
- Writes own name, even if imperfectly
- Tells a story with a clear sequence

**Activities**
- Play letter-sound “I spy” games
- Practice writing name on different surfaces (chalk, whiteboard, sand)
- Take turns adding the next part of a shared story
- Play simple sound-blending games with 2–3 sounds

### Math


**Milestones**
- Counts to 20+ with accuracy
- Writes some numerals
- Performs simple addition/subtraction with objects (within 5)
- Compares quantities using “more/fewer/equal”
- Recognizes and extends simple patterns

**Activities**
- Play “how many more?” games with small object sets
- Try simple board or dice games involving counting
- Extend patterns with beads, blocks, or stickers
- Practice writing numerals

### Science


**Milestones**
- Describes simple life cycles with support (seed to plant, egg to chick)
- Identifies seasons and associated weather/clothing
- Makes simple predictions before an experiment
- Asks increasingly detailed questions

**Activities**
- Sequence life-cycle picture cards (butterfly, plant, frog)
- Take seasonal nature walks and keep a weather journal
- Predict-and-test experiments (will it sink or float? guess first!)
- Care for a class or home plant or pet together

### Social Studies


**Milestones**
- Names their neighborhood or town
- Understands basic rules and why they matter
- Begins to recognize cultural traditions and celebrations
- Shows growing empathy and cooperative play

**Activities**
- Take a neighborhood walk and talk about local landmarks
- Discuss family or cultural celebrations and traditions
- Play cooperative games that require sharing and turn-taking
- Practice simple “helping others” tasks at home

### Technology & Logic


**Milestones**
- Follows multi-step directions independently
- Solves simple logic puzzles (what comes next in a sequence)
- Begins to understand basic unplugged “coding” concepts (a sequence of steps)
- Shows early digital-citizenship awareness (asking before using a device)

**Activities**
- Play unplugged coding games — give step-by-step directions to “move like a robot” through an obstacle course
- Try logic puzzles and simple mazes
- Have age-appropriate digital-safety conversations (“ask a grown-up first”)
- Play pattern and sequence games

### Storybooks


**Recommended**
Longer picture books with more complex narratives; alphabet and counting books; early nonfiction picture books about animals, transportation, or nature to build background knowledge.
