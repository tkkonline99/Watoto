## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Science |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 8–9 (Grade 3) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Investigations with variables and evidence-based conclusions; builds on Grade 2 habitats and matter |
| Standards Alignment | US: NGSS 3-LS1 to 3-LS4 / 3-PS2 / 3-ESS2-3  ·  Canada (ON): Grade 3 Science  ·  Global: Cambridge Primary Science Stage 3–4 |

## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 3 Science year.

### Unit 1: Life Cycles & Ecosystems (Weeks 1–9)

- Describe life cycles of various organisms and how they differ
- Understand how living things depend on each other in an ecosystem
- Describe food chains (producer, consumer, decomposer)
- Explain how changes to an ecosystem affect living things
Key vocabulary: ecosystem, food chain, producer, consumer, decomposer

### Unit 2: Forces & Motion (Weeks 10–18)

- Understand that forces can be balanced or unbalanced
- Describe how force affects the motion of an object
- Investigate friction and gravity
- Predict motion based on forces acting on an object
Key vocabulary: force, balanced, unbalanced, friction, gravity

### Unit 3: Weather & Climate (Weeks 19–27)

- Distinguish between weather and climate
- Describe how weather is measured and predicted
- Understand how climate varies in different regions
- Interpret weather data and simple climate patterns
Key vocabulary: weather, climate, region, precipitation, meteorologist

### Unit 4: Day, Night & the Moon (Weeks 28–36)

- Explain that Earth's rotation causes day and night
- Describe the pattern of the phases of the moon
- Understand that the sun, moon, and stars appear to move in patterns
- Observe and record patterns in the sky
Key vocabulary: rotation, orbit, phase, pattern

## 3. Unit 1: Life Cycles & Ecosystems


### Lesson Plan — “Everything is Connected”


**Objective**
Students will describe food chains and how living things depend on each other in an ecosystem.

**Materials**
Ecosystem picture cards, food chain arrow cards, chart paper.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What would happen to a rabbit if all the grass disappeared?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce ecosystem parts: producers (plants), consumers (animals), decomposers (fungi, bacteria). Build a simple food chain together (grass → rabbit → fox), using arrows to show energy flow.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children build a food chain from picture cards.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What happens to the food chain if one part is removed?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on a simple 3-part food chain. Stretch: build a food web with multiple connections.

## 4. Unit 2: Forces & Motion


### Lesson Plan — “Balanced and Unbalanced Forces”


**Objective**
Students will understand balanced vs. unbalanced forces and investigate friction and gravity.

**Materials**
A ball, a toy car, different surfaces (carpet, tile), a rope for tug-of-war demonstration.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Do a quick tug-of-war demo; ask when the rope moves and when it doesn't.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce balanced forces (equal, no motion change) vs. unbalanced (unequal, causes motion). Investigate friction by rolling a car on different surfaces; discuss gravity pulling things down.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children test how far a car rolls on different surfaces and discuss friction.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What force slows the car down on carpet?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on push/pull and friction. Stretch: introduce measuring distances and comparing forces quantitatively.

### Practice Worksheet

## 5. Unit 3: Weather & Climate


### Lesson Plan — “Weather vs. Climate”


**Objective**
Students will distinguish between weather and climate and interpret weather data.

**Materials**
Weather data charts, a world climate map, weather tool pictures (thermometer, rain gauge).

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What's the difference between what it's like today and what it's usually like here?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Explain weather (day-to-day conditions) vs. climate (typical pattern over a long time). Introduce weather tools and how meteorologists predict weather. Discuss how climate differs by region.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children interpret a simple weather data chart and describe patterns.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Is a snowy day in a warm climate weather or climate?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the weather/climate distinction. Stretch: compare climates of two different regions using data.

## 6. Unit 4: Day, Night & the Moon


### Lesson Plan — “Earth, Sun, and Moon Patterns”


**Objective**
Students will explain that Earth's rotation causes day and night and describe moon phases.

**Materials**
A globe or ball for Earth, a flashlight for the sun, a small ball for the moon, moon phase pictures.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why do we have day and night?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Use a globe and flashlight to demonstrate that Earth's rotation causes day and night (the side facing the sun has day). Show moon phase pictures and discuss the repeating pattern.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children model day/night with a globe and light, and sequence moon phase cards.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What causes the pattern of day and night?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on day/night rotation. Stretch: introduce that the moon orbits Earth and phases repeat about every month.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 3 Science year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Describes food chains and how living things depend on each other
- Understands balanced/unbalanced forces, friction, and gravity
- Distinguishes weather from climate and interprets weather data
- Explains day/night from Earth's rotation and describes moon phases

**Question Types to Expect**
Food chain building, force and motion questions, weather/climate distinction, and Earth-sun-moon pattern questions with diagrams.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Observe local wildlife and talk about food chains
- Experiment with rolling objects on different surfaces
- Track the weather and talk about your region's climate
- Watch the moon over a month and note how it changes

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
