# SOCIAL STUDIES — GRADE 1 (AGES 6–7)

Complete Curriculum Package — All 4 Units
Master Curriculum Project

## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Social Studies |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 6–7 (Grade 1) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Expands outward from the Kindergarten focus on self and family to community, geography, and a first sense of history |
| Standards Alignment | US: C3 Framework Grade 1  ·  Canada (ON): Grade 1 Social Studies  ·  Global: IB PYP / Cambridge Global Perspectives |
| Assessment Approach | Worksheet → quiz → unit test per unit, plus one cumulative exam guide for parents/teachers |


## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 1 Social Studies year.

### Unit 1: Community Roles & Jobs (Weeks 1–9)

- Identify different jobs and roles in a community and what they do
- Explain how community helpers help others
- Understand that people depend on each other in a community
- Identify a job they might like to have someday
Key vocabulary: community, job, role, helper, depend

### Unit 2: Needs vs. Wants (Weeks 10–18)

- Distinguish between needs (food, water, shelter, clothing) and wants
- Explain why needs must be met before wants
- Sort items into needs and wants categories
- Understand that people and families make choices about needs and wants
Key vocabulary: need, want, choice, shelter

### Unit 3: Maps & Globes (Weeks 19–27)

- Identify the difference between a map and a globe
- Locate basic features on a simple map (land, water, roads)
- Begin using basic directions on a map
- Use a simple map to find a location
Key vocabulary: map, globe, land, water, direction

### Unit 4: Past & Present (Weeks 28–36)

- Compare life today to life in the past using pictures and artifacts
- Identify simple changes in technology, clothing, or transportation over time
- Understand that stories and objects from the past teach us about history
- Interview a family member about ‘the past’
Key vocabulary: past, present, history, artifact, change

## 3. Unit 1: Community Roles & Jobs


### Lesson Plan — “Who Helps Our Community?”


**Objective**
Students will identify community helpers and explain how their jobs help others.

**Materials**
Community helper picture cards (doctor, firefighter, teacher, mail carrier, farmer), simple job-tool matching cards.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Who are some people who help us in our community?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce community helper cards one at a time, discussing what each person does and how they help others.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children match community helpers to their tools or workplaces (firefighter → fire truck, doctor → stethoscope).

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask children which community helper job they'd like to try and why.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on 3–4 very familiar helpers. Stretch: discuss how multiple helpers might work together, such as during an emergency.

## 4. Unit 2: Needs vs. Wants


### Lesson Plan — “Needs and Wants”


**Objective**
Students will distinguish between needs and wants and explain why needs come first.

**Materials**
Picture cards of needs (food, water, shelter, clothing) and wants (toys, candy, video games), two sorting mats.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What are some things you can't live without?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the difference between needs (things we must have to survive) and wants (things that are nice to have but not necessary). Sort a few example cards together.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children sort a new set of picture cards into needs/wants mats.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “If you only had a little money, would you buy a need or a want first? Why?”

**Differentiation**
Support: use very clear-cut examples (food vs. toy). Stretch: discuss trickier examples and why some items might be both.

## 5. Unit 3: Maps & Globes


### Lesson Plan — “Maps and Globes”


**Objective**
Students will identify the difference between a map and a globe and locate basic features on a simple map.

**Materials**
A globe, a simple world or neighborhood map, picture cards showing land and water.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a globe and a map side by side. Ask: “What's the same? What's different?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Explain that a globe is a round model of the whole Earth, while a map is a flat picture of a place. Point out land and water on both.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children point to land and water areas on a simple map, and practice finding their country or region on a globe (with help).

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “When might you use a map? When might you use a globe?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on identifying land vs. water only. Stretch: introduce basic cardinal directions (N/S/E/W) using a compass rose.

## 6. Unit 4: Past & Present


### Lesson Plan — “Then and Now”


**Objective**
Students will compare life today to life in the past and understand how objects and stories teach us about history.

**Materials**
Pictures showing ‘then’ and ‘now’ (old cars vs. new cars, old phones vs. new phones, old vs. new clothing), a simple family interview question sheet.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a picture of an old telephone; ask “What is this? Do we still use this today?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Compare pairs of then/now pictures (transportation, communication, clothing), discussing what has changed and why.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, sort a set of ‘then’ and ‘now’ picture cards into two categories.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What is one thing you think might be different 50 years from now?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on one category only, such as transportation. Stretch: have children interview a family member about something that was different when they were young.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 1 Social Studies year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Identifies community helpers and their roles
- Distinguishes needs from wants
- Understands the difference between maps and globes and reads basic map features
- Compares past and present and understands how we learn about history

**Question Types to Expect**
Matching and circling, sorting, true/false, simple map reading, comparison questions, and short explanations.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Point out community helpers you encounter in daily life
- Talk through family spending decisions in age-appropriate ways (needs vs. wants)
- Use real maps or GPS together and talk about what you see
- Look at old family photos and share stories about ‘the past’

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
