## About This Guide

This is a dedicated, deeper companion to the combined Early Years guide, focused on the final preschool year before Kindergarten. It is organized by the same six subjects the app uses, and each subject includes a ‘bridge to Kindergarten’ note connecting it to the Kindergarten packages in this curriculum. Learning at this age is still play-based and hands-on — not worksheets or tests — but with a gentle emphasis on the skills and independence that make starting school a joyful, confident experience.

## Developmental Snapshot (Ages 4–5)

Every child develops at their own pace — these are general patterns, not a checklist to rush. Children do not need to master everything here before Kindergarten.

**Body & movement**
- Hops on one foot, begins to skip, and catches a ball
- Cuts along a line with scissors and draws a person with several features
- Writes some letters and their own name, and manages buttons and zippers

**Communication & language**
- Speaks in complex sentences and tells detailed stories
- Knows most letters and many letter sounds, and enjoys rhyming
- Asks many questions and follows multi-step directions

**Social & emotional**
- Plays cooperatively, forms close friendships, and follows rules
- Manages feelings better and can wait, share, and negotiate
- Shows independence in self-care (dressing, toileting, tidying up)

**Thinking & learning**
- Counts to twenty or beyond and recognizes numerals and quantities
- Knows letters and sounds and may begin to read a few familiar words
- Sorts, classifies, sequences, and does simple adding with objects

## 1. Early Language & Literacy

This is the app’s ‘Reading & Writing’ subject. In the pre-K year, children connect letters to sounds, hear the parts of words, and use writing and drawing to communicate — the direct on-ramp to reading.

**Learning goals**
- Knows most letters and many letter sounds
- Hears rhymes, syllables, and beginning sounds in words
- Writes their name and some letters, and labels drawings

**Play-based activities**
- Play letter-sound games (‘What starts with /m/? Mmmm-mommy, mmmm-milk!’)
- Clap the syllables in names and words (‘di-no-saur’)
- Make up rhymes together and think of words that rhyme
- Practice writing their name and other meaningful letters
- Encourage your child to ‘label’ their drawings with letters or words
- Retell stories with a clear beginning, middle, and end

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child linking letters to sounds, hearing rhymes, and using letters to communicate. These are the strongest predictors of reading readiness.

**Bridge to Kindergarten**
This leads directly into the Reading & Writing — Kindergarten package, which begins formal phonics, letter formation, and simple sentence writing.

## 2. Early Numeracy & Thinking

This is the app’s ‘Math’ subject. Pre-K children count larger sets accurately, recognize and write some numerals, and begin simple addition and subtraction with real objects.

**Learning goals**
- Counts to twenty or beyond with accuracy
- Recognizes numerals and matches them to quantities
- Adds and takes away small amounts using objects

**Play-based activities**
- Count collections of objects and write the numeral for how many
- Solve ‘how many altogether?’ and ‘how many are left?’ with toys or snacks
- Extend more complex patterns (red, red, blue, red, red, blue…)
- Make a simple picture graph (favorite fruits in the family)
- Measure things with cubes or blocks (‘the book is 6 blocks long’)
- Practice writing numerals in sand, with chalk, or in the air

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child counting accurately, recognizing numerals, and combining or separating small groups. Understanding ‘how many’ matters more than rote counting.

**Bridge to Kindergarten**
This leads directly into the Math — Kindergarten package, which builds number sense to 20, addition and subtraction within 10, and shapes.

## 3. Exploring the World

This is the app’s ‘Science’ subject. Pre-K children investigate, predict, and begin to record what they observe — thinking and working like young scientists.

**Learning goals**
- Makes predictions and tests simple ideas
- Records observations by drawing
- Sorts living and non-living things and uses the five senses

**Play-based activities**
- Keep a simple science journal — draw a plant or the moon over several days
- Sort pictures or objects into ‘living’ and ‘non-living’
- Take a five-senses walk and describe what you notice
- Do simple experiments and guess the outcome first (‘Will the ice melt faster in the sun?’)
- Investigate how things grow and change (plants, weather, seasons)
- Ask ‘what do you think will happen?’ and ‘why do you think that?’

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child predicting, observing closely, and recording ideas through drawing. Encouraging curiosity and ‘science talk’ is the best preparation.

**Bridge to Kindergarten**
This leads directly into the Science — Kindergarten package, which formalizes the five senses, living things, weather, and simple investigations.

## 4. Me & My World

This is the app’s ‘Social Studies’ subject. Pre-K children build the self-awareness, friendship skills, and sense of community that support both school and life.

**Learning goals**
- Names feelings and uses simple calm-down strategies
- Cooperates, shares, and resolves small conflicts with support
- Understands family, community, and that people are similar and different

**Play-based activities**
- Practice calm-down strategies together (deep breaths, counting, a quiet corner)
- Play cooperative games where everyone works toward one goal
- Explore community helpers and how people depend on each other
- Draw a simple map of the classroom, home, or neighborhood
- Talk about ‘same and different’ among people, families, and cultures with respect
- Introduce needs vs. wants in everyday choices

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child managing feelings, cooperating, and showing empathy. These social-emotional skills are among the strongest predictors of school success.

**Bridge to Kindergarten**
This leads directly into the Social Studies — Kindergarten package, which builds on self, family, community roles, and getting along with others.

## 5. Cause, Effect & Problem-Solving

This is the app’s ‘Technology & Logic’ subject. Pre-K logic means ‘unplugged’ coding (sequences and simple algorithms), sorting, patterns, and the very first, carefully guided technology skills.

**Learning goals**
- Creates and follows step-by-step sequences (simple algorithms)
- Sorts, classifies, and solves simple logic puzzles
- Begins basic device skills and the ‘ask a grown-up first’ habit

**Play-based activities**
- Play unplugged coding: use arrow cards to ‘program’ a friend to walk to a target
- Put the steps of a daily routine in the right order
- Sort objects by rules and then re-sort them a different way
- Solve simple mazes and logic puzzles together
- Introduce careful, supervised device basics (tapping, simple navigation)
- Establish the ‘ask a grown-up before using something new’ rule from the start

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child ordering steps, following and giving directions, and sorting by rules. This logical, step-by-step thinking is the foundation of coding.

**Bridge to Kindergarten**
This leads directly into the Technology & Logic — Kindergarten package, which builds sequencing, simple patterns/algorithms, and first online-safety habits.

## 6. Books & Stories

This is the app’s ‘Storybooks’ subject. Pre-K children understand story elements and begin to predict and infer — pre-reading skills that make learning to read easier.

**Learning goals**
- Identifies characters, setting, and the story’s problem
- Predicts and makes simple inferences
- Retells stories and chooses favorite books

**Play-based activities**
- Talk about the characters, setting, and problem in a story
- Pause to predict (‘what do you think she’ll do?’) and infer (‘how do you think he feels?’)
- Retell a story in order using pictures or your own words
- Explore different kinds of books — stories, poems, and information books
- Let your child ‘read’ familiar books from memory and pictures
- Visit the library and let your child choose books to fall in love with

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your child discussing story parts, predicting, and ‘reading’ familiar books. This comprehension and love of stories powers early reading.

**Bridge to Kindergarten**
This leads directly into the Storybooks — Kindergarten reading guide and the reading skills in the Kindergarten Reading & Writing package.

## School Readiness Checklist (For Parents & Teachers)

Readiness is about much more than letters and numbers. This gentle checklist highlights the independence, attention, and social skills that help children thrive in Kindergarten. Children develop these at different rates — use it to celebrate growth and spot areas to support through play, not as a pass-or-fail test.

**Independence & self-care**
- Uses the toilet independently and washes hands
- Puts on a coat and shoes with little help
- Tidies up and takes care of belongings

**Attention & following directions**
- Follows two- to three-step directions
- Sits and focuses on an activity for a short while
- Transitions between activities with support

**Social & emotional**
- Separates from caregivers with growing confidence
- Plays and shares with other children
- Expresses needs with words and manages feelings with support

**Early academic**
- Recognizes many letters and some sounds; recognizes their written name
- Counts and recognizes numerals; knows shapes and colors
- Holds a crayon or pencil and enjoys drawing and mark-making

## Everyday Routines & Caregiver Tips

In the pre-K year, confidence and independence matter as much as academics.
- Build independence: let your child dress, pack, and tidy up themselves, even if it takes longer
- Establish predictable routines and a calm bedtime for enough sleep
- Weave letters, sounds, and numbers into daily life — no worksheets needed
- Coach problem-solving and feelings rather than fixing everything for your child
- Read together every day and talk about the stories — the single best readiness activity

## When to Check with a Professional

Children this age vary widely, and readiness skills develop on different timelines. Still, trust your instincts and talk with your pediatrician or your child’s future school if, over time, your child: is very hard for others to understand; cannot follow simple two-step directions; shows little interest in letters, numbers, or books; struggles greatly to separate, share, or manage feelings; or seems to lose skills. Early support, when needed, makes a real difference — and asking is always okay.
