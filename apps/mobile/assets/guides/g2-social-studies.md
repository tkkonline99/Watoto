Complete Curriculum Package — All 4 Units
Master Curriculum Project

## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Social Studies |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 7–8 (Grade 2) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Widens from local community to continents, cultures, and citizenship, with a first look at local history |
| Standards Alignment | US: C3 Framework Grade 2  ·  Canada (ON): Grade 2 Social Studies  ·  Global: IB PYP / Cambridge Global Perspectives |
| Assessment Approach | Worksheet → quiz → unit test per unit, plus one cumulative exam guide for parents/teachers |


## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 2 Social Studies year.

### Unit 1: Local History (Weeks 1–9)

- Understand that communities have a history and change over time
- Identify how the local community has changed
- Recognize important people or events in local history
- Use simple timelines to show sequence
Key vocabulary: history, community, timeline, change, past

### Unit 2: Continents & Oceans (Weeks 10–18)

- Identify the seven continents and five oceans on a map or globe
- Understand that people live all around the world
- Locate their own country on a world map
- Compare different places around the world
Key vocabulary: continent, ocean, globe, country, world

### Unit 3: Citizenship & Rules (Weeks 19–27)

- Understand what it means to be a good citizen
- Explain why communities have rules and laws
- Identify rights and responsibilities of citizens
- Describe ways to help their community
Key vocabulary: citizen, rule, law, right, responsibility

### Unit 4: Cultures Around the World (Weeks 28–36)

- Recognize that different cultures have different traditions, foods, and celebrations
- Compare cultural practices respectfully
- Understand that culture is passed down through families and communities
- Appreciate diversity in their community and the world
Key vocabulary: culture, tradition, diversity, celebration, respect

## 3. Unit 1: Local History


### Lesson Plan — “Our Community Then and Now”


**Objective**
Students will understand that communities change over time and use a simple timeline to show sequence.

**Materials**
Then/now photos of a local area (or generic town), a blank timeline template.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Do you think our town always looked the way it does now?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Compare then/now images of a community, discussing what has changed (buildings, transportation, population). Introduce a simple timeline showing a few key dates.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, place 3–4 events on a timeline in order.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What is one way our community has changed over time?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on obvious visual changes with picture support. Stretch: have children interview a family member about how the community has changed.

## 4. Unit 2: Continents & Oceans


### Lesson Plan — “Our Big World”


**Objective**
Students will identify the seven continents and five oceans and locate their own country on a world map.

**Materials**
A world map, a globe, continent/ocean labels, a blank world map worksheet.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Spin a globe and ask: “How many big pieces of land do you think there are in the world?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the seven continents and five oceans, pointing to each on a map and globe. Locate the children's own country.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children label continents and oceans on a blank map with support.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask children to name the continent they live on.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on identifying own continent and a few major ones. Stretch: have children name an animal or landmark associated with different continents.

## 5. Unit 3: Citizenship & Rules


### Lesson Plan — “Being a Good Citizen”


**Objective**
Students will understand what it means to be a good citizen and why communities have rules and laws.

**Materials**
Scenario cards (good citizenship examples), chart paper for rights and responsibilities.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What does it mean to be a good person in your community?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Discuss what a citizen is and what makes someone a good citizen (following rules, helping others, being respectful). Explain why rules and laws help everyone. Introduce rights (things everyone deserves) and responsibilities (things we should do).

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, sort scenario cards into ‘good citizen’ and ‘not a good citizen’ and discuss.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What is one way you can be a good citizen?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on concrete examples of good citizenship. Stretch: discuss the difference between a rule and a law, and why we need both.

## 6. Unit 4: Cultures Around the World


### Lesson Plan — “Celebrating Cultures”


**Objective**
Students will recognize that different cultures have different traditions and compare them respectfully.

**Materials**
Books or pictures showing cultural traditions, foods, and celebrations from around the world; a simple culture-comparison chart.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What is a special food or celebration in your family?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Explore a few cultures from around the world (foods, clothing, celebrations, music), discussing similarities and differences. Emphasize that culture is passed down through families.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children compare two cultural traditions, naming one similarity and one difference.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why is it good that the world has so many different cultures?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on one or two clearly different cultures with picture support. Stretch: have children research and present a tradition from a culture different from their own.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 2 Social Studies year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Understands communities have a history and uses simple timelines
- Identifies the seven continents and five oceans and locates their own country
- Understands citizenship, rules, rights, and responsibilities
- Recognizes and respects different cultures and traditions

**Question Types to Expect**
Sequencing on timelines, map labeling, matching and circling, true/false, comparison questions, and short explanations.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Share stories about how your community or family has changed
- Explore a world map or globe together at home
- Talk about being a good citizen in everyday situations
- Celebrate and learn about different cultures through food, books, and events

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
