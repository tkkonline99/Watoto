# AGES 2–3 — BABIES & TODDLERS

Development & Play Guide
Master Curriculum Project — Early Years

## About This Guide

This is a dedicated, deeper companion to the combined Early Years guide, focused on the toddler years. It is organized by the same six subjects the app uses, but toddlers learn through play, movement, repetition, and lots of language — not lessons or tests. This is the age of the ‘word explosion,’ the first pretend play, and big feelings, so patience and play are your best teaching tools.

## Developmental Snapshot (Ages 2–3)

Every toddler develops at their own pace — these are general patterns, not a checklist to rush.

**Body & movement**
- Walks, runs, climbs, and begins to jump with two feet
- Kicks and throws a ball, and walks up and down stairs with help
- Stacks several blocks, scribbles with crayons, and starts to use a spoon and fork

**Communication & language**
- Has a rapid ‘word explosion,’ growing from a few words to hundreds
- Puts two words together around age two (‘more milk’) and short sentences by age three
- Follows simple instructions and loves to ask ‘what’s that?’

**Social & emotional**
- Plays alongside other children (‘parallel play’) more than with them
- Has big feelings and tantrums — this is normal, as self-control is only beginning
- Imitates others, shows early empathy, and wants to do things ‘all by myself’

**Thinking & exploring**
- Begins pretend play (feeding a doll, ‘talking’ on a toy phone)
- Sorts objects by color or shape and completes simple puzzles
- Follows two-step directions and understands ideas like ‘big’ and ‘little’

## 1. Early Language & Literacy

This is the app’s ‘Reading & Writing’ subject. Toddlers are soaking up language at an astonishing rate. Talking, singing, rhyming, and reading together fuel vocabulary and lay the groundwork for reading.

**Simple learning goals**
- Uses more words and begins combining them
- Enjoys interactive book-sharing and songs
- Makes first marks and scribbles with crayons

**Play-based activities**
- Name everything — objects, actions, feelings — to grow vocabulary (‘You’re jumping!’)
- Sing songs with rhyme and repetition (‘Wheels on the Bus,’ ‘Twinkle Twinkle’)
- Ask simple questions during play (‘Where’s the doggy?’) and wait for a response
- Read short, repetitive, predictable books and let your toddler chime in
- Offer chunky crayons for free scribbling — this is early writing
- Describe pictures in books and connect them to your child’s life

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice new words appearing and your toddler’s attempts to join in with familiar songs and books. Every new word is a milestone worth celebrating.

## 2. Early Numeracy & Thinking

This is the app’s ‘Math’ subject. Toddlers begin to count by rote, notice shapes and sizes, and sort objects — the building blocks of mathematical thinking, all through play.

**Simple learning goals**
- Begins to say number words in order (‘one, two, three’)
- Sorts and matches objects by color or shape
- Understands comparisons like big/little and more/less

**Play-based activities**
- Count everyday things together — stairs as you climb, snacks on a plate
- Sort toys, socks, or blocks by color into piles
- Play with shape sorters and simple inset puzzles
- Compare sizes (‘the big ball and the little ball’) during play
- Practice sharing with ‘one for you, one for me’ to build one-to-one ideas
- Use positional words in play (‘in’ the box, ‘under’ the table, ‘on top’)

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your toddler starting to sort, match, and use number words — even if counting isn’t accurate yet, the interest in patterns and quantity is what matters.

## 3. Exploring the World

This is the app’s ‘Science’ subject. Toddlers are enthusiastic, messy investigators. Sensory play, water, sand, and nature let them explore how the world works.

**Simple learning goals**
- Explores materials with hands and tools
- Notices cause and effect (‘I pour, it splashes’)
- Shows curiosity about animals, plants, and weather

**Play-based activities**
- Set up a supervised sensory bin (dry rice, pasta, or water) with cups and scoops
- Go on nature walks and collect leaves, stones, and sticks to sort
- Explore floating and sinking with safe objects in the bath (never leave your toddler alone in water)
- Mix a little water and washable paint to see colors change
- Plant a seed in a cup and watch it grow over days
- Make animal sounds together and point out animals in books and outdoors

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice what fascinates your toddler and let them repeat it — repetition is how young children make sense of the world.

## 4. Me & My World

This is the app’s ‘Social Studies’ subject. Toddlers are learning about feelings, family, and getting along with others — skills that grow through pretend play and gentle guidance.

**Simple learning goals**
- Names some feelings and recognizes them in others
- Engages in simple pretend and imitation play
- Begins to understand family and familiar routines

**Play-based activities**
- Name feelings as they happen (‘You’re frustrated because the tower fell’)
- Encourage pretend play — feeding a doll, ‘cooking,’ caring for a stuffed animal
- Talk about family members and look at family photos together
- Point out community helpers (mail carrier, doctor) in books and life
- Give simple ‘jobs’ like putting toys in a bin to build belonging
- Model and gently coach sharing and taking turns (it’s just beginning at this age)

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your toddler naming feelings and imitating everyday life in play — this pretend play is how they process and understand their world.

## 5. Cause, Effect & Problem-Solving

This is the app’s ‘Technology & Logic’ subject. At this age it means hands-on problem-solving and simple sequencing — not screens. Toddlers love figuring out how things fit and work.

**Simple learning goals**
- Completes simple puzzles and shape sorters
- Understands simple sequences (‘first… then…’)
- Solves small problems through trial and error

**Play-based activities**
- Offer simple 2–4 piece puzzles and shape sorters
- Build a tower together and knock it down — cause and effect in action
- Use ‘first… then…’ language in routines (‘First shoes, then outside’)
- Play sorting games (put all the red blocks here, blue blocks there)
- Let your toddler work at small challenges (opening a container) before helping
- Keep any screen time tiny, high-quality, and shared — real play teaches logic best at this age

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your toddler persisting at a puzzle or figuring out how something opens. That determination and trial-and-error is early logical thinking.

## 6. Books & Stories

This is the app’s ‘Storybooks’ subject. Toddlers love the same books over and over — repetition builds language and comfort. Book time is interactive and joyful.

**Simple learning goals**
- Enjoys favorite books repeatedly
- Turns pages and points to pictures
- Begins to ‘fill in’ words in familiar stories

**Play-based activities**
- Choose predictable, repetitive, and lift-the-flap books
- Read the same favorites again and again — toddlers thrive on repetition
- Pause before a familiar word and let your toddler fill it in
- Let your toddler turn the pages and point to pictures they know
- Ask ‘what’s that?’ and name pictures together
- Act out simple stories (stomp like the animal, wave bye-bye)

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice your toddler choosing favorite books and joining in with familiar lines. This love of stories is the strongest predictor of later reading enjoyment.

## Everyday Routines & Caregiver Tips

Toddlers learn best through consistent routines, lots of language, and patience.
- Keep predictable routines — toddlers feel secure knowing what comes next
- Narrate and expand on what your toddler says (‘Ball!’ → ‘Yes, a big red ball!’)
- Offer simple choices (‘apple or banana?’) to support their growing independence
- Stay calm during tantrums — big feelings are normal and your steadiness helps them settle
- Allow plenty of active, hands-on play and time outdoors every day

## Safety & Wellbeing

Curious, mobile toddlers need a safe space to explore.
- Keep small objects, button batteries, and magnets out of reach — choking and swallowing risks remain high
- Supervise closely near water, stairs, streets, and cooking areas
- Anchor heavy furniture and TVs to the wall to prevent tip-overs as toddlers climb
- Store cleaning products and medicines locked and out of reach
- Provide a safe, cushioned space for active climbing and jumping

## When to Check with a Professional

Toddlers vary widely, and a single ‘late’ skill is usually fine. Still, trust your instincts and talk with your pediatrician or health visitor if, over time, your toddler: is not using single words by around 18 months or two-word phrases by around age two; does not seem to understand simple instructions; makes little eye contact or shows little interest in other people; is not walking by around 18 months; or seems to lose skills they once had. Early support, when needed, makes a meaningful difference.
