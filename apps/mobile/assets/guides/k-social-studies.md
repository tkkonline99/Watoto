# SOCIAL STUDIES — KINDERGARTEN (AGES 5–6)

Complete Curriculum Package — All 4 Units
Master Curriculum Project

## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Social Studies |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 5–6 (Kindergarten) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Discussion, sharing, and cooperative play; concepts grounded in the child's own family, classroom, and neighborhood |
| Standards Alignment | US: state-led, C3 Framework as reference  ·  Canada (ON): Grade K Social Studies  ·  Global: IB PYP ‘Who We Are’ / Cambridge Global Perspectives |
| Assessment Approach | Worksheet → quiz → unit test per unit, plus one cumulative exam guide for parents/teachers |


## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Kindergarten Social Studies year.

### Unit 1: All About Me & My Family (Weeks 1–9)

- Describe self using basic characteristics (name, age, family)
- Identify family members and their roles
- Recognize that families come in many forms
- Share something unique about their family
Key vocabulary: family, self, unique, similar, different

### Unit 2: Our Classroom Community & Rules (Weeks 10–18)

- Identify classroom rules and explain why they matter
- Describe jobs and responsibilities within a classroom community
- Practice fair ways to solve simple disagreements
- Understand what it means to be a good community member
Key vocabulary: rule, community, fair, responsibility, cooperate

### Unit 3: Mapping My World (Weeks 19–27)

- Understand that a map represents a real place
- Identify basic locations on a simple classroom or neighborhood map
- Use positional words (near, far, next to) to describe locations
- Create a simple map of a familiar place
Key vocabulary: map, location, near, far, neighborhood

### Unit 4: Celebrating Our Differences (Weeks 28–36)

- Recognize that people celebrate different traditions and holidays
- Identify similarities and differences between families and cultures
- Show respect for differences in others
- Share a family tradition or celebration
Key vocabulary: tradition, celebration, culture, respect, similar

## 3. Unit 1: All About Me & My Family


### Lesson Plan — “All About My Family”


**Objective**
Students will describe their family and recognize that families look different from one another.

**Materials**
Family photos or drawings, a simple “All About Me” worksheet template, chart paper.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Go around the circle; each child says their name and one family member's name.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Share family photos or drawings; each child tells one thing about their family (who lives with them, a favorite family activity).

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, create a chart showing the different family sizes and types represented in the room, discussing that all families are valued.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What is one thing that makes your family special?”

**Differentiation**
Support: provide a sentence frame (“My family has ___ people.”). Stretch: ask children to compare their family to a classmate's, noting similarities and differences respectfully.

## 4. Unit 2: Our Classroom Community & Rules


### Lesson Plan — “Our Classroom Rules”


**Objective**
Students will identify classroom rules, explain their purpose, and practice fair ways to resolve disagreements.

**Materials**
Chart paper, markers, simple scenario cards (e.g., “Two friends want the same toy”).

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What is a rule? Can you think of a rule we follow at home?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
As a class, brainstorm and write down classroom rules (raise your hand, share, clean up). Discuss why each rule helps everyone.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
Act out a simple scenario card (two children want the same toy); discuss fair solutions together (take turns, use a timer).

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask children to name one classroom rule and why it's important.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on 2–3 core rules with pictures. Stretch: have children help create a new class rule and explain their reasoning.

## 5. Unit 3: Mapping My World


### Lesson Plan — “Making a Map of Our Classroom”


**Objective**
Students will understand that a map represents a real place and create a simple map using positional words.

**Materials**
A simple existing map (of the school or neighborhood), large paper, crayons.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a simple map; ask “What do you think this picture shows?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Walk around the classroom together, naming key areas (reading corner, door, teacher's desk). Discuss how a map could show these from above.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, begin drawing a simple map of the classroom on chart paper, placing key items with positional words (the door is near the hallway, the reading corner is next to the window).

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet (draw a simple map).

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Children point to one item on their map and describe its location using a positional word.

**Differentiation**
Support: provide a partially completed map template to fill in. Stretch: have children map a slightly larger familiar space, like their bedroom or backyard.

## 6. Unit 4: Celebrating Our Differences


### Lesson Plan — “Celebrating Our Differences”


**Objective**
Students will identify that people celebrate different traditions and practice showing respect for those differences.

**Materials**
Books or pictures showing different cultural celebrations, a simple “share a tradition” prompt sheet.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What is your favorite holiday or celebration?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Look at pictures or a book showing a few different cultural celebrations. Discuss what's similar (people gather, special food, happiness) and what's different (specific customs).

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In small groups, children share a family tradition or celebration with each other.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why is it good that people celebrate in different ways?”

**Differentiation**
Support: provide picture prompts for children who need help expressing a tradition. Stretch: have children compare two celebrations and name one similarity and one difference.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

Keep this low-stress and conversational — most answers here are personal and open-ended by design. This guide covers the full Kindergarten Social Studies year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Describes self and family, and recognizes that families differ
- Identifies classroom rules and practices fair conflict resolution
- Understands that maps represent real places and uses positional words
- Recognizes and shows respect for different traditions and celebrations

**Question Types to Expect**
Open personal reflection, matching or circling, true/false, drawing with labels, and simple explanations.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Talk about family stories and traditions at home
- Involve kids in creating or discussing household rules
- Point out maps in everyday life (GPS, store directories, park signs)
- Share books or media about diverse families and traditions

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
