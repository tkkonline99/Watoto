| Subject | Science |

| Age Band / Stage | 9–10 (Grade 4) |

## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 4 Science year, the final band in this curriculum.

### Unit 1: Energy & Simple Machines (Weeks 1–9)

- Understand that energy can be transferred and transformed
- Identify forms of energy (kinetic, potential, heat, light, sound, electrical)
- Understand how simple machines make work easier
- Investigate energy transfer in a system
Key vocabulary: energy, kinetic, potential, transfer, simple machine

### Unit 2: Food Webs & Energy Transfer (Weeks 10–18)

- Understand how energy flows through a food web
- Trace energy from the sun through producers to consumers
- Understand the roles of organisms in an ecosystem
- Explain how changes affect the whole food web
Key vocabulary: food web, energy flow, producer, consumer, decomposer

### Unit 3: Erosion & the Rock Cycle (Weeks 19–27)

- Understand how weathering and erosion change Earth's surface
- Describe the rock cycle and the three rock types
- Understand how rocks form and change over time
- Interpret evidence of Earth's changes in rock layers
Key vocabulary: weathering, erosion, rock cycle, sedimentary, igneous, metamorphic

### Unit 4: The Solar System (Weeks 28–36)

- Identify the planets and their order from the sun
- Understand Earth's place in the solar system
- Explain the relationship between the sun, Earth, and moon
- Understand why we have seasons
Key vocabulary: solar system, planet, orbit, rotation, season

## 3. Unit 1: Energy & Simple Machines


### Lesson Plan — “Energy on the Move”


**Objective**
Students will identify forms of energy and understand how simple machines make work easier.

**Materials**
A ball, a ramp, examples of simple machines (lever, pulley, inclined plane), a flashlight.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Roll a ball down a ramp; ask what made it move.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce forms of energy (kinetic = motion, potential = stored, plus heat, light, sound, electrical) and energy transfer. Introduce simple machines (lever, pulley, inclined plane, wheel and axle, wedge, screw) and how they make work easier.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children identify forms of energy in examples and match simple machines to their uses.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What form of energy does a flashlight give off?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on kinetic vs. potential and one or two machines. Stretch: trace energy transformations through a system (e.g., battery → flashlight → light + heat).

## 4. Unit 2: Food Webs & Energy Transfer


### Lesson Plan — “Energy Through the Web”


**Objective**
Students will understand how energy flows through a food web, starting with the sun.

**Materials**
Food web diagram, organism cards, arrow cards.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Where does the energy in food originally come from?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Trace energy from the sun → producers (plants) → consumers (animals) → decomposers. Build a food web (not just a chain) showing multiple connections. Discuss how removing one organism affects the whole web.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children build a food web from organism cards and trace energy flow.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What happens to the food web if the plants die?”

**Differentiation**
Support: use a simple food web with a few organisms. Stretch: predict multiple effects of removing an organism from the web.

## 5. Unit 3: Erosion & the Rock Cycle


### Lesson Plan — “Rocks That Change”


**Objective**
Students will understand weathering, erosion, and the rock cycle.

**Materials**
Rock samples (or pictures) of the three types, a tray with sand/water for erosion, rock cycle diagram.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a smooth river rock; ask how it got so smooth.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce weathering (breaking rock down) and erosion (moving the pieces). Introduce the three rock types (sedimentary, igneous, metamorphic) and the rock cycle showing how rocks change over time.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children sequence the rock cycle and sort rock types.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How can one type of rock become another?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on weathering/erosion and identifying rock types. Stretch: explain the full rock cycle in their own words.

## 6. Unit 4: The Solar System


### Lesson Plan — “Our Place in Space”


**Objective**
Students will identify the planets and understand Earth's place in the solar system.

**Materials**
Solar system diagram/model, planet cards, a light for the sun and balls for Earth/moon.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How many planets are there, and where is Earth?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the eight planets in order from the sun. Explain Earth's place, the sun as the center, and how Earth orbits the sun while rotating. Explain that the tilt of Earth causes seasons.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children order the planets and model Earth's orbit and rotation.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why do we have seasons?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on planet order and Earth's rotation/orbit. Stretch: explain how Earth's tilt causes seasons in more detail.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 4 Science year (all 4 units) — the final band in the curriculum.

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Identifies forms of energy and how simple machines make work easier
- Traces energy flow through a food web from the sun
- Understands weathering, erosion, and the rock cycle
- Identifies planets and explains Earth's place and why we have seasons

**Question Types to Expect**
Energy and simple machine questions, food web tracing, rock cycle and erosion questions, and solar system questions with diagrams.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Point out simple machines and energy transfers at home
- Discuss local food webs and ecosystems
- Collect and identify rocks and talk about how they formed
- Stargaze and talk about the planets and seasons together

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
