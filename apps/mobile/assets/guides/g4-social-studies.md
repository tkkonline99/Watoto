Complete Curriculum Package — All 4 Units
Master Curriculum Project

## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Social Studies |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 9–10 (Grade 4) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Focuses on the student's own province/state — its history, geography, government, and economy — a common Grade 4 anchor |
| Standards Alignment | US: C3 Framework Grade 4 (often state history)  ·  Canada (ON): Grade 4 Social Studies (regions/early settlement)  ·  Global: IB PYP / Cambridge Global Perspectives |

## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 4 Social Studies year, the final band in this curriculum. Because Grade 4 often centers on the student's own province or state, this package is written so it can be applied to whichever province/state or region the student lives in.

### Unit 1: Provincial / State History (Weeks 1–9)

- Understand key events in the history of one's own province or state
- Identify important people and groups, including Indigenous peoples and early settlers
- Use timelines to sequence provincial/state history
- Understand how the past shaped the present region
Key vocabulary: province/state, Indigenous, settler, timeline, heritage

### Unit 2: Government Structures (Weeks 10–18)

- Understand the branches or levels of government in one's country
- Explain how laws are made
- Identify the roles of key leaders and representatives
- Understand rights and responsibilities of citizens
Key vocabulary: branch, legislature, representative, law, citizen

### Unit 3: Regions, Resources & Environment (Weeks 19–27)

- Identify the physical regions and natural resources of one's province/state
- Understand how geography and resources affect where and how people live
- Explain how people use and can protect natural resources
- Understand human impact on the environment
Key vocabulary: region, natural resource, environment, conservation

### Unit 4: Trade & Economy Introduction (Weeks 28–36)

- Understand how goods and services move within and between regions
- Explain the concepts of imports and exports
- Understand supply, demand, and how prices are set (basic level)
- Recognize how people's jobs connect to the wider economy
Key vocabulary: trade, import, export, supply, demand, economy

## 3. Unit 1: Provincial / State History


### Lesson Plan — “The Story of Our Region”


**Objective**
Students will understand key events and people in their province's or state's history.

**Materials**
Timeline template, images of local historical figures and Indigenous peoples, local history resources.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Who lived in our region long before it looked the way it does today?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Discuss the history of the local province/state: Indigenous peoples who lived there first, early settlers, and key events. Build a timeline of a few major events. Emphasize respect for Indigenous history and heritage.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children sequence historical events on a timeline.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet (adaptable to their region).

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How did events in the past shape our region today?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on a few clear events and the timeline. Stretch: research one historical figure or group and present their significance.

## 4. Unit 2: Government Structures


### Lesson Plan — “How Our Government Works”


**Objective**
Students will understand the levels or branches of government and how laws are made.

**Materials**
Diagram of the country's government structure, examples of leaders/representatives, a 'how a law is made' flowchart.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Who makes the laws for our country, and how?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce the structure of the country's government (branches or levels, representatives, leaders). Walk through a simplified version of how a law is made. Review citizens' rights and responsibilities.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children put the steps of making a law in order.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why is it important that citizens can take part in government?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the basic idea of representatives and law-making. Stretch: compare the roles of different levels or branches of government.

## 5. Unit 3: Regions, Resources & Environment


### Lesson Plan — “Land, Resources, and People”


**Objective**
Students will identify their region's resources and understand human impact on the environment.

**Materials**
Regional map, pictures of natural resources, examples of conservation.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What natural resources come from our region?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Identify the physical regions and natural resources of the province/state. Discuss how geography and resources shape where and how people live. Introduce human impact on the environment and ways to conserve and protect resources.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children match resources to how people use them and brainstorm conservation ideas.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why is it important to protect natural resources?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on identifying resources and simple conservation. Stretch: analyze a specific environmental issue in the region and propose solutions.

## 6. Unit 4: Trade & Economy Introduction


### Lesson Plan — “How Goods and Money Move”


**Objective**
Students will understand how goods and services move within and between regions, including imports and exports.

**Materials**
Map showing trade routes, examples of imported/exported goods, a simple supply/demand example.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Where do the things we buy actually come from?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Explain how goods and services move within and between regions. Introduce imports (goods brought in) and exports (goods sent out). Introduce basic supply and demand and how it affects prices. Connect jobs to the wider economy.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children sort goods into 'made here' vs. 'imported' and discuss why.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why might a country import some goods and export others?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on imports/exports and goods movement. Stretch: work through a simple supply-and-demand price scenario.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 4 Social Studies year (all 4 units) — the final band in the curriculum. Because Grade 4 often focuses on the student's own province or state, adapt the specifics to your region.

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Understands provincial/state history, including Indigenous peoples and settlers
- Understands government structures and how laws are made
- Identifies regions and resources and understands human environmental impact
- Understands trade, imports/exports, and basic supply and demand

**Question Types to Expect**
Regional history and timelines, government and civics questions, resources and environment questions, and trade/economics questions.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Visit local history museums or landmarks together
- Discuss current events and how government decisions affect your family
- Talk about where household goods come from and local resources
- Explore your region's geography on maps and in person

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
