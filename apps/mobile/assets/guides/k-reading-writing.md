# READING & WRITING — KINDERGARTEN (AGES 5–6)

Complete Curriculum Package — All 4 Units
Master Curriculum Project

## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Reading & Writing |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 5–6 (Kindergarten) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Oral language and phonemic awareness first; print concepts and writing build alongside, through play, read-alouds, and short structured practice |
| Standards Alignment | US: Common Core RF.K / RL.K / W.K  ·  Canada (ON): Grade K Language  ·  Global: Cambridge Primary English Stage 1 / IB PYP |
| Assessment Approach | Worksheet → quiz → unit test per unit, plus one cumulative exam guide for parents/teachers |


## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Kindergarten Reading & Writing year, moving from letters and sounds through decoding, sight words and sentences, to stories and retelling.

### Unit 1: Letters & Sounds (Weeks 1–9)

- Recognize and name all 26 uppercase and lowercase letters
- Produce the primary sound for each consonant and short vowel
- Recognize own name and other familiar words
- Distinguish between letters, words, and sentences
Key vocabulary: letter, sound, uppercase, lowercase, alphabet, vowel, consonant

### Unit 2: Blending & Decoding (Weeks 10–18)

- Blend individual sounds to read simple CVC words (e.g., c-a-t → cat)
- Segment simple CVC words into individual sounds
- Read a short list of decodable CVC words
- Begin to self-monitor when a word doesn't make sense
Key vocabulary: blend, segment, decode, CVC word, rhyme

### Unit 3: Sight Words & Sentences (Weeks 19–27)

- Read and write a core set of high-frequency sight words (the, is, a, I, see, my, like, and)
- Write a simple sentence with a capital letter and end punctuation
- Use finger spacing between words when writing
- Begin to apply phonics knowledge when writing (invented spelling)
Key vocabulary: sight word, sentence, capital letter, period, space

### Unit 4: Stories & Retelling (Weeks 28–36)

- Listen to a story and retell it in order (beginning, middle, end)
- Identify the characters and setting in a story
- Answer simple who/what/where questions about a text
- Draw and label a picture to tell a simple story
Key vocabulary: character, setting, beginning, middle, end, retell

## 3. Unit 1: Letters & Sounds


### Lesson Plan — “Letter Sound Scavenger Hunt”


**Objective**
Students will identify the primary sound of at least 10 target letters and match them to pictures beginning with that sound.

**Materials**
Alphabet cards (uppercase and lowercase), picture cards for common objects (apple, ball, cat, dog, egg, fish), a small basket or bag.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Sing the alphabet song while pointing to letters on a wall chart.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Hold up a letter card and say its sound aloud (“b says /b/”). Show a picture card (ball) and ask if it starts with that sound. Repeat for 8–10 letters.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
Children sort a small pile of picture cards into groups by beginning sound, placing each under the matching letter card.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Show one new picture card; children say the beginning sound and try to name the matching letter.

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on 5–6 highly familiar letters/sounds — letters in the child's own name are a good start. Stretch: introduce letter pairs that look or sound similar (b/d, m/n) for extra discrimination practice.

## 4. Unit 2: Blending & Decoding


### Lesson Plan — “Building CVC Words”


**Objective**
Students will blend three individual sounds to read simple CVC words.

**Materials**
Magnetic letters or letter cards (a, e, i, o, u, and common consonants), a small whiteboard, CVC word picture cards.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Practice “robot talk” — the teacher says a word in segmented sounds (c-a-t) and children blend it together and say the whole word.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Model building a CVC word with letter cards (c-a-t), sliding a finger under each letter while blending the sounds, then reading the whole word.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children build 4–5 CVC words from a teacher-given list using letter cards, reading each one aloud after building it.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Show a CVC word card; children blend and read it aloud together.

**Differentiation**
Support: start with words that begin with continuous sounds (m, s, f), which are easier to blend (“sat,” “man”). Stretch: introduce simple CVCC or CCVC words (“nest,” “frog”) for children ready for more challenge.

## 5. Unit 3: Sight Words & Sentences


### Lesson Plan — “Sight Word Sentences”


**Objective**
Students will read a set of core sight words and use them to write a simple sentence with correct spacing and end punctuation.

**Materials**
Sight word flashcards (the, is, a, I, see, my, like, and), sentence strips or lined paper, pencils or crayons.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Flash sight word cards quickly; children read each word aloud as a group (“sight word speed round”).

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Model writing a simple sentence using sight words and a picture word (“I see a cat.”), pointing out the capital letter at the start, finger-space between words, and period at the end.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, build 2–3 sentences together using sight word and picture cards, checking capital letter, spacing, and period each time.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Children read their own sentence from the worksheet aloud to a partner.

**Differentiation**
Support: provide a sentence frame (“I see a ___.”) for children to fill in with a picture word. Stretch: ask children to write two connected sentences about the same picture.

## 6. Unit 4: Stories & Retelling


### Lesson Plan — “Retelling Our Story”


**Objective**
Students will retell a short story in order (beginning, middle, end) and identify the main character and setting.

**Materials**
A short, simple picture book, story-sequence picture cards (or blank paper divided into 3 boxes), crayons.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask children to recall a favorite story and name one character from it.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Read a short story aloud. Afterward, ask: “Who was the story about? Where did it happen? What happened first? What happened next? What happened at the end?”

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, sequence 3 picture cards from the story in order (beginning, middle, end) and retell the story together using the pictures as prompts.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet (draw-and-label retelling).

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
A few children retell the story in 1–2 sentences to the group using their drawings.

**Differentiation**
Support: retell with only 2 parts (beginning/end) and heavy picture support. Stretch: add a fourth “problem and solution” element to the retelling.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

As with Math, keep this low-stress and playful for 5–6 year olds — short sessions, lots of read-aloud time, and celebration of effort. This guide covers the full Kindergarten Reading & Writing year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Recognizes and names all uppercase and lowercase letters
- Produces the primary sound for each letter
- Blends and reads simple CVC words
- Reads a core set of sight words (the, is, a, I, see, my, like, and)
- Writes a simple sentence with a capital letter and end punctuation
- Retells a story in order (beginning, middle, end) and identifies character/setting

**Question Types to Expect**
Letter and sound identification, CVC word building and reading, sight word recognition, simple sentence writing, and story retelling/comprehension questions.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Read aloud together every day, pointing to words as you read
- Practice letter sounds during everyday moments (“I spy something that starts with /b/”)
- Keep writing low-pressure — invented spelling (like “kat” for “cat”) is a normal, healthy part of learning
- Celebrate attempts at reading and writing, not just correct answers

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
