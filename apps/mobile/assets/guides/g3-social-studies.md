# SOCIAL STUDIES — GRADE 3 (AGES 8–9)

Complete Curriculum Package — All 4 Units
Master Curriculum Project

## 1. Program Overview

| Subject | Social Studies |
|---|---|
| Age Band / Stage | 8–9 (Grade 3) |
| Duration | Full school year — approximately 36 weeks across 4 units |
| Learning Approach | Introduces how communities and governments work, geography of regions, and the flow of goods and services |
| Standards Alignment | US: C3 Framework Grade 3  ·  Canada (ON): Grade 3 Social Studies  ·  Global: IB PYP / Cambridge Global Perspectives |
| Assessment Approach | Worksheet → quiz → unit test per unit, plus one cumulative exam guide for parents/teachers |


## 2. Scope & Sequence

Four units cover the full Grade 3 Social Studies year.

### Unit 1: Communities & Government Basics (Weeks 1–9)

- Understand why communities need government
- Identify basic levels of government (local, state/provincial, national)
- Describe some services governments provide
- Understand how leaders are chosen (voting/elections)
Key vocabulary: government, leader, election, vote, service

### Unit 2: Regions & Geography (Weeks 10–18)

- Identify different types of regions (landforms, climate, culture)
- Use map features (compass rose, key/legend, scale)
- Understand how geography affects how people live
- Locate major geographic features of their country
Key vocabulary: region, compass rose, legend, scale, geography

### Unit 3: Local & National History (Weeks 19–27)

- Identify important events and people in local and national history
- Understand how the past shapes the present
- Use timelines to sequence historical events
- Recognize symbols and traditions of their country
Key vocabulary: history, timeline, symbol, tradition, event

### Unit 4: Goods, Services & Economy (Weeks 28–36)

- Distinguish between goods and services
- Understand producers and consumers
- Explain how people earn, spend, and save money
- Understand basic supply and demand in simple terms
Key vocabulary: goods, services, producer, consumer, supply, demand

## 3. Unit 1: Communities & Government Basics


### Lesson Plan — “Why We Have Government”


**Objective**
Students will understand why communities need government and identify basic levels of government.

**Materials**
Chart showing local/state/national levels, pictures of government services, voting simulation materials.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Who makes the rules for our town? For our whole country?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Explain that government helps organize communities, make rules/laws, and provide services (schools, roads, safety). Introduce local, state/provincial, and national levels. Discuss how leaders are elected by voting.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
As a class, hold a simple vote on a fun classroom decision to model elections.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 1 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why is it important that people can vote for their leaders?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the idea of government providing services and rules. Stretch: discuss the roles of specific leaders (mayor, governor/premier, president/prime minister).

## 4. Unit 2: Regions & Geography


### Lesson Plan — “Reading Maps and Regions”


**Objective**
Students will use map features and understand how geography affects how people live.

**Materials**
Maps with compass rose, legend, and scale; region examples.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Show a map; ask what the different symbols and colors might mean.

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce map features: compass rose (directions), legend/key (what symbols mean), scale (distance). Discuss different kinds of regions (by landform, climate, or culture) and how geography shapes daily life.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children use a map's legend and compass rose to answer questions.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 2 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How might living near mountains be different from living near the ocean?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on the compass rose and legend. Stretch: use map scale to estimate distances between two points.

## 5. Unit 3: Local & National History


### Lesson Plan — “How the Past Shapes Us”


**Objective**
Students will identify important historical events and understand how the past shapes the present.

**Materials**
Timeline template, pictures of national symbols and historical figures, local history examples.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What are some symbols that represent our country?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Discuss important events and people in local and national history. Introduce national symbols and traditions. Use a timeline to place a few key events in order and discuss how the past affects today.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children sequence historical events on a timeline.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 3 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “How does something from the past still affect us today?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on recognizing symbols and using a simple timeline. Stretch: research one historical figure and explain their importance.

## 6. Unit 4: Goods, Services & Economy


### Lesson Plan — “Goods, Services, and Money”


**Objective**
Students will distinguish goods from services and understand producers, consumers, and basic economics.

**Materials**
Picture cards of goods and services, play money, a simple supply/demand example.

**Warm-Up (5 minutes)**
Ask: “What's the difference between buying an apple and paying someone to cut your hair?”

**Main Activity (15 minutes)**
Introduce goods (things you can touch) vs. services (work someone does for you). Discuss producers (who make/provide) and consumers (who buy). Introduce earning, spending, and saving, plus simple supply and demand.

**Guided Practice (10 minutes)**
In pairs, children sort picture cards into goods and services.

**Independent Practice (10 minutes)**
Children complete the Unit 4 practice worksheet.

**Wrap-Up / Check for Understanding (5 minutes)**
Ask: “Why might something cost more if lots of people want it but there isn't much of it?”

**Differentiation**
Support: focus on goods vs. services. Stretch: discuss a simple supply-and-demand scenario and its effect on price.

## 7. Cumulative Exam & Assessment Guide (For Parents & Teachers)

This guide covers the full Grade 3 Social Studies year (all 4 units).

**End-of-Year Skills Checklist**
- Understands why communities need government and identifies its levels
- Uses map features and understands how geography affects life
- Identifies historical events and understands how the past shapes the present
- Distinguishes goods from services and understands basic economics

**Question Types to Expect**
Government and civics questions, map-feature use, timelines and history questions, and goods/services sorting with economics questions.

**Tips for Supporting Your Child**
- Talk about local government and voting in age-appropriate ways
- Use maps on trips and point out compass directions and legends
- Share family and community history stories
- Involve kids in simple budgeting and saving conversations

**Suggested Pacing (Full Year)**
Each unit follows: Worksheet → 2–3 days of practice → Quiz → about 1 week more practice → Unit Test → move to the next unit, across roughly 36 weeks.
