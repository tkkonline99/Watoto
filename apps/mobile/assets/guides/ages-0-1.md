# AGES 0–1 — BABIES & TODDLERS

Development & Play Guide
Master Curriculum Project — Early Years

## About This Guide

This is a dedicated, deeper companion to the combined Early Years Milestones & Activities Guide, focused entirely on the first year of life. It is organized by the same six subjects the app uses, but everything at this age happens through relationships, play, and everyday routines — not lessons or tests. Babies learn from warm, responsive interaction (often called ‘serve and return’): you respond to their sounds and cues, and that back-and-forth builds their brain.

## Developmental Snapshot (Ages 0–1)

Every baby grows at their own pace — these are general patterns across the first year, not a schedule to rush.

**Body & movement**
- Lifts head during tummy time, then rolls over, sits with and then without support
- Begins to crawl or scoot, pulls up to stand, and may take first steps around the first birthday
- Reaches for and grasps objects, passes them between hands, and develops the finger-and-thumb ‘pincer’ grip

**Communication & language**
- Coos and then babbles (‘ba-ba,’ ‘da-da’), and takes turns making sounds with you
- Responds to their name and to familiar voices, and understands simple words like ‘no’ and ‘bye-bye’
- Uses gestures like waving or reaching, and may say one or two first words near age one

**Social & emotional**
- Smiles socially, enjoys faces, and laughs during play
- Shows stranger awareness and may become upset when a caregiver leaves (this is a sign of healthy attachment)
- Seeks comfort and calms when held — responding to their needs builds security, you cannot ‘spoil’ a baby with love

**Thinking & exploring**
- Explores objects with hands and mouth, and repeats actions that cause a reaction (like dropping a toy)
- Begins to understand that objects still exist when hidden (this is why peekaboo is so fascinating)
- Looks for a dropped or hidden toy — an early sign of memory and problem-solving

## 1. Early Language & Literacy

This is the app’s ‘Reading & Writing’ subject, at its very beginning. For babies, literacy means bathing them in warm language: talking, singing, and sharing books builds the foundation for all future reading and writing.

**Simple learning goals**
- Responds to your voice and turns toward familiar sounds
- Babbles and ‘takes turns’ in sound conversations with you
- Enjoys looking at simple books and being sung to

**Play-based activities**
- Narrate your day out loud (‘Now we’re putting on your socks’) so baby hears lots of words
- Sing nursery rhymes and lullabies — rhythm and rhyme build early language
- When baby babbles, babble back and pause — this teaches the rhythm of conversation
- Share sturdy cloth or board books daily, even for just a minute or two; let baby touch and mouth them
- Point to and name objects, body parts, and people (‘That’s your nose!’)
- Play peekaboo and name what happens (‘Where’s baby? There you are!’)

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice whether baby turns toward your voice, makes eye contact, and begins to babble. Sharing these moments matters far more than any ‘result.’

## 2. Early Numeracy & Thinking

This is the app’s ‘Math’ subject, in its earliest form. Babies aren’t counting yet, but they are building the thinking skills numbers rest on: noticing objects appear and disappear, filling and emptying, and exploring how things fit together.

**Simple learning goals**
- Reaches for and explores objects of different sizes
- Enjoys filling and emptying containers
- Begins to look for hidden objects (early object permanence)

**Play-based activities**
- Count fingers and toes gently during play and diaper changes (baby hears number words)
- Offer stacking rings or nesting cups to explore size and order
- Play fill-and-dump with a container and safe objects (large enough not to be a choking hazard)
- Hide a toy under a cloth and find it together (‘all gone… here it is!’)
- Use routine words like ‘all done,’ ‘more,’ and ‘again’ that underlie early math ideas
- Let baby explore how objects go ‘in’ and ‘out’ of a box

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice baby’s curiosity about where things go and delight in finding hidden objects — these are the seeds of mathematical thinking.

## 3. Exploring the World

This is the app’s ‘Science’ subject, at the sensory stage. Babies are natural scientists: they investigate the world through their senses and by acting on objects to see what happens.

**Simple learning goals**
- Explores different textures, sounds, and sights
- Repeats actions to make something happen (cause and effect)
- Shows interest in nature and new surroundings

**Play-based activities**
- Offer a ‘texture basket’ of safe materials (soft cloth, crinkly paper, smooth wood) to touch — always supervised
- Enjoy gentle water play in the bath (never leave baby alone in water, even for a second)
- Blow bubbles and watch baby track them with their eyes
- Take baby outside to feel a breeze, see leaves move, and hear birds
- Provide safe cause-and-effect toys (press a button, hear a sound) once baby can grasp
- Use an unbreakable mirror so baby can discover their own reflection

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice what captures baby’s attention — a sound, a texture, a moving object. Following their interest is the heart of early science.

## 4. Me & My World

This is the app’s ‘Social Studies’ subject, beginning with the most important relationship: the bond between baby and caregiver. Social learning at this age is about feeling safe, loved, and connected.

**Simple learning goals**
- Recognizes familiar faces and voices
- Enjoys simple social games
- Seeks and accepts comfort from caregivers

**Play-based activities**
- Play face-to-face games like peekaboo and pat-a-cake
- Name family members while looking at photos together
- Make faces in a mirror together and name feelings (‘happy!’)
- Respond warmly and promptly to baby’s cries — this builds trust and security
- Sing social songs with gentle touch (‘This Little Piggy’)
- Give lots of cuddles, eye contact, and smiles throughout the day

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice how baby lights up for familiar people and calms with comfort. This secure bond is the foundation for all later social and emotional growth.

## 5. Cause, Effect & Problem-Solving

This is the app’s ‘Technology & Logic’ subject, at the pre-technology stage. There are no screens or coding here — just the earliest logic: figuring out that actions have effects, and working out how to reach a goal.

**Simple learning goals**
- Repeats an action to get a reaction (early cause and effect)
- Works to reach or retrieve a desired toy
- Explores how simple objects work

**Play-based activities**
- Offer simple cause-and-effect toys (shake for a rattle sound, press for a pop-up)
- Play dropping games — baby drops a toy, you return it (this is problem-solving and cause-effect, even if repetitive!)
- Place a favorite toy just out of reach and cheer as baby works to get it
- Provide containers to open, close, fill, and empty
- Roll a ball back and forth to show action and reaction
- Avoid screens at this age (other than video calls with loved ones) — hands-on play is how babies learn logic

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice baby experimenting — shaking, banging, dropping, reaching. This trial-and-error is the very beginning of logical thinking.

## 6. Books & Stories

This is the app’s ‘Storybooks’ subject. Book-sharing at this age is a cozy, sensory, bonding experience — not about ‘reading’ the words or finishing the story.

**Simple learning goals**
- Enjoys being held and sharing a book
- Looks at high-contrast pictures
- Explores books by touching and mouthing them

**Play-based activities**
- Choose sturdy board books, cloth books, and touch-and-feel books
- Share high-contrast black-and-white books, which newborns see most easily
- Keep sessions short and frequent — a minute here and there is perfect
- Let baby hold, chew, and turn pages; books are toys at this age
- Point to and name one picture per page rather than reading every word
- Use a warm, sing-song voice and let baby set the pace

**What to notice (gentle observation)**
Notice whether baby enjoys the closeness and looks at the pictures. A love of books grows from these warm, pressure-free moments.

## Everyday Routines & Caregiver Tips

For babies, the richest learning happens in ordinary moments, not special activities.
- Talk, sing, and narrate throughout feeding, changing, and bath time
- Give plenty of supervised ‘tummy time’ while baby is awake to build strong muscles
- Follow baby’s cues for play and rest — babies need lots of downtime
- Respond consistently to build security; a predictable, loving routine is deeply reassuring
- Limit background TV and adult screen use during interaction, which can distract from serve-and-return

## Safety & Wellbeing

Safe exploration is essential at this stage.
- Always place baby on their back to sleep, on a firm surface with no loose bedding, pillows, or toys
- Supervise tummy time and all play; never leave baby alone with small objects
- Keep small parts, cords, and anything smaller than a toilet-paper tube away — choking is a serious risk
- Never leave baby alone in or near water, even for a moment
- Secure furniture and cover outlets as baby becomes mobile

## When to Check with a Professional

Babies develop on their own timelines, and a single ‘late’ skill is usually nothing to worry about. Still, trust your instincts and talk with your pediatrician or health visitor if you notice, over time, that your baby: does not respond to loud sounds; does not make eye contact or smile socially; is not babbling by around 12 months; is not sitting with support by around 9 months; or seems to lose skills they once had. Early support, when needed, makes a real difference — and asking is always okay.
