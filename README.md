# Watoto

**Watoto** is a multilingual (English / Swahili / French) learning app for young children, built in Flutter for Android, iOS, Web and Windows.

The curriculum covers ages 0–10, and **the build ships all of it**. Every stage now meets the depth its design tier calls for; the flag that held the upper stages back ([docs/V1_SCOPE.md](docs/V1_SCOPE.md)) is fully open.

This repository holds two different things, and it matters which is which:

- **The app that exists** — `apps/mobile`, and the content and media it plays. It runs.
- **The system that is designed but not built** — the 25 documents in `docs/`, plus `backend/` and `apps/api`. These are drawings and code that no shipped build talks to.

## Launch status — what actually runs today

**Watoto is a single-device offline app.** Everything a child or parent does happens on the phone. There is no server in the loop.

| Area | Real state |
|------|-----------|
| Child + parent app | **Working.** 151 files in `lib`, 74 test files / 475 passing tests, `flutter analyze` clean of warnings and errors. Android / Web / Windows build; iOS needs a Mac or cloud runner. |
| Age range shipped | **Ages 0–10 (stages 1–10)** — the whole curriculum. `maxPublishedStage` was raised one stage at a time as each reached depth, and is now at 10 — [docs/V1_SCOPE.md](docs/V1_SCOPE.md). |
| Content | **684 items** (342 lessons, 228 quizzes, 114 stories) across 10 stages, fully localized EN/SW/FR. Every subject has the build plan's 6 lessons, 4 quizzes and 2 stories, all at their tier's depth — see *Content depth* below. |
| Illustration | **838 bundled PNGs**, generated by `apps/mobile/tool/generate_art_batch.py`. Of the 2,140 slot visuals the library declares, 628 have a file and **1,512 do not** and render as the striped placeholder carrying the brief. See *Why the missing art is not generated* below. |
| Narration | **On-device TTS by default.** A 7,983-file pre-rendered VO pack (EN/SW/FR) exists and is *not* bundled — see *Media* below. |

### Content depth

Breadth is real; depth is arriving stage by stage, in the order
`_buildPlan.priorityOrder` asks for.

**Depth is measured per design tier, on all three item types.** Steps per
lesson: `parent-led` 4, `encourage-effort` 6, `gamify-gently` and
`grant-autonomy` 8. Questions per quiz: 2 / 3 / 4 / 4. Pages per story:
4 / 5 / 6 / 6. A stage-7 teaching arc is the wrong shape for a one-year-old.
`test/lesson_depth_test.dart` holds all three lines and fails if any stage is
flattened.

The quiz and story minimums arrived five stages late, and that is worth
knowing: "stage N is done" had been read off lesson depth alone, so stages 1–5
were finished while 172 of 199 quizzes sat at one question and **every story in
the library** sat at exactly three pages. A ratchet that guards one of three
item types certifies one third of the work.

**The library is finished against its own build plan.** All ten stages are at
the depth their design tier calls for, on all three item types. That took seven
passes, in `_buildPlan.priorityOrder`: stages 6 and 7 first, then 1, then 4 and
5, then 2 and 3, then 8, 9 and 10. Not one quiz asks a single question any more
and not one story stops at three pages. All three ratchets in
`test/lesson_depth_test.dart` now read zero, which is the end of that road
rather than a milestone to relax — they may no longer fall, and any rise is a
stage being flattened.

The two bands read differently, deliberately. **Stages 1–3 are `parent-led`**
(ages 0–3): 4 steps, 2 questions, 4 pages, on an infant arc — notice, do, do
again changed, then carry it into the room. The grown-up is the user. **Stages
4–5 run the 6-step `encourage-effort` arc** at 3 questions and 5 pages, with
the child as the user. **Stages 6 and 7 are the deepest in the library** at
about two and two and a half hours of play, on the `gamify-gently` arc, which
adds a worked example and a check-yourself step because a six-year-old can
follow one and a four-year-old cannot. Stage 7's ten pilot lessons
(`content/deepen_stage7.py`) were left untouched by `author_stage7.py`, which
deepened the other 26 around them.

Two things worth knowing about how the quiz and story depth was filled in.
Every shipped question and page was **carried over word for word in all three
languages** rather than rewritten — a working translation is not worth
discarding to reword a line. And the two item types needed opposite treatment:
quizzes were *appended* to, because a question stands alone, while stories had
to be *rebuilt*, because each three-pager put its ending on the last page and
depth there meant inserting the middle beat, not adding two pages after the
close.

**Stages 8, 9 and 10 had never been in a build at all.** Between them: 108
lessons two or three steps deep, 54 quizzes asking one or two questions, 18
stories at three pages. All three now run the `grant-autonomy` arc — 8 steps,
4 questions, 6 pages — built around a step the younger bands cannot use:
*check yourself*, which hands the child a plausible wrong answer and asks them
to find the fault in it, rather than telling them they were right. Stage 10's
coding lessons run real Python, so nothing appended to them declares `codeRun`
— the mini-editor is built by parsing English narration, and a Swahili child
would get a different editor from the same JSON.

**One mechanism was deleted rather than kept.** `quiz_review.dart` used to top
short quizzes up by borrowing authored recap questions from nearby lessons —
written when 140 of 166 quizzes asked a single question. With every quiz now
authored to its tier, it fired for nothing, and a borrowed recap was always the
weaker question: it came from a lesson the child may not have played, so a
wrong answer could mean "never taught" rather than "not learned". Removing it
changed no behaviour; it had already stopped running.

Remaining across the library: **nothing**. Every gap the worklist tracks —
items to author, items below their tier's depth — is zero. What it still
reports is 58 items with no drawn illustration, which fall back to an emoji
chosen from the step text, and 77 declared audio cues that were never recorded.
Both are media gaps, not authoring ones. The per-subject worklist is
[docs/CONTENT_WORKLIST.md](docs/CONTENT_WORKLIST.md), regenerated by
`node content/worklist.js`.

That worklist used to open with "138 lessons with no recap", which was noise.
A recap is a written multiple-choice question, so every `reading: false` lesson
withholds one deliberately — and the split turned out to be exact: all 204
reading lessons have a recap, none of the 138 pre-reading ones do. It now
counts only reading lessons, and the figure is 0. Whether a pre-reader should
get a *spoken* two-option recap is a real design question, since the recap view
narrates as well as prints; it is not 138 pieces of missing content.

Authored by `content/author_stage{1..10}.py`, all idempotent with a `--check`
mode, sharing `content/authoring.py`. A stage costs roughly 3,000–3,900 lines
of authoring script and 2,700–2,900 new strings across the three languages.

### Why the missing art is not generated

`generate_art_batch.py` will happily draw all 1,512, and it should not be asked
to. It classifies a brief by keyword and falls through to an abstract pattern
when nothing matches. Run over the undrawn slots it produces a picture for 68%
of them, but "recognised" is not "right": a rain cloud for *"Is an apple a fruit
or a grain?"*, a night sky for *"start at the bigger number and count on"*, a
coloured blob for *"Move right, then jump"*. The remaining 32% are blobs
outright.

That is the fault the whole visual contract exists to prevent — the same shape
as "Put a lid on a pan of hot water" rendering a tin of food, which is what
`docs/26` was written after. A striped placeholder says "this is not drawn yet"
and prints a true description; a wrong picture says "this is finished" and
misleads a child who is looking at it for a clue. Filling the slots would make
`noart` read zero and make the app worse.

The classifier was rewritten rather than left as it was
(`apps/mobile/tool/brief_classifier.py`, tested by `test_brief_classifier.py`).
It matches whole words instead of substrings, scores every motif and takes the
best rather than the first rule written, and — the part that matters — returns
None when it cannot place a brief, so `generate_art_batch.py` writes no file
and the placeholder stays. It is confident about 44% of the undrawn slots and
declines the rest, where the old cascade drew something for 66% and was wrong
or blank-eyed for about half of that.

So the tool is now safe to run, and running it would fill roughly 670 of the
1,512. That is still a decision rather than an obligation: these are shapes on
a pastel ground, not illustration, and a subject-appropriate shape is a
different thing from a good picture. What is settled is that it will no longer
draw a rain cloud over a question about fruit.

The rest need an illustrator. Until then the placeholder is the honest state.

## One source of truth

`git` is it. This repo is the only place work should live.

Historically `C:\dev\watoto` was a **robocopy mirror** used to dodge Windows Defender Controlled Folder Access (below), and the two copies diverged badly: 564 files existed only in the mirror — the whole media pipeline, release tooling, and nine features — while newer work existed only here. A copy in the wrong direction had also overwritten the mirror's shared plumbing, leaving those nine features referencing store and l10n APIs that no longer existed anywhere. All of it is now merged into this history.

`C:\dev\watoto` is now a **clone**, not a mirror. Sync is git:

```powershell
# in C:\dev\watoto (the build clone) — day-to-day work happens here
git pull                  # take changes made in the OneDrive repo
git push                  # send them back; the OneDrive working tree updates itself
                          # (receive.denyCurrentBranch=updateInstead is set there)
```

**Never robocopy between the two again.** A file copy cannot tell which side is newer; that is what caused the divergence above.

```powershell

# Android (SDK at C:\dev\android-sdk, JDK 21 at C:\dev\jdk-21):
```

### Building Android on this machine

`flutter build apk` works, but **must be invoked via `tool/build-android.ps1`** (or with
`TMP`/`TEMP` set to a clean path like `C:\dev\tmp`). Reason: this machine's endpoint
security blocks AF_UNIX socket files under the default `%TEMP%`
(`C:\Users\...\AppData\Local\Temp`). Gradle's Java NIO selector opens such a socket as an
internal self-pipe, so a plain build dies instantly with *"Unable to establish loopback
connection"* (`UnixDomainSockets.connect0 → Invalid argument: connect`). Redirecting
`TMP`/`TEMP` elsewhere sidesteps it — no admin rights needed. This is **not** an iOS/Android
code issue and does not affect CI or other machines.

iOS still requires macOS + Xcode (or a cloud-Mac CI runner) — unchanged.

> **⚠ Windows note (this machine):** Windows Defender **Controlled Folder Access** blocks
> `dart.exe` (and slows `node.exe`) inside the OneDrive folder, so `flutter` cannot build or
> test there. Day-to-day work happens in the clone at **`C:\dev\watoto`** and moves by
> `git pull` / `git push` — see *One source of truth* above. **Do not robocopy**; that is
> what silently split this project in two.
>
> Permanent fix, in order of preference: allowlist
> `C:\dev\flutter\bin\cache\dart-sdk\bin\dart.exe` under Windows Security → Ransomware
> protection → *Allow an app through Controlled folder access* (needs admin), or move the
> repo out of OneDrive entirely and keep OneDrive for the VO backup only.

### What a child and parent can actually do

Onboarding in EN/SW/FR → child profiles behind an FTC-style parent gate → a savanna home with
subject islands, a daily challenge, XP, coins, streaks and a mascot → a player that runs
lessons, quizzes and stories from `watoto_content_library.json` with tap / drag / count /
choice / trace / code-block step types, illustrations, SFX, narration and captions → an
end-of-lesson recap question → badges, avatars and savanna decorations bought with coins →
a Parent Zone with weekly progress, curriculum guides, screen-time settings, practice
reminders, data export, backup/restore, diagnostics and legal pages.

Underneath: a Hive-backed offline store, an idempotent sync queue that survives restarts
(tested, but with nowhere to drain), an adaptive resolver that offers an easier or harder
item based on recent attempts, and a spaced-review ladder that brings back what a child
missed.

## Non-Negotiable Product Constraints

Enforced by the app today:

1. **Child safety**: no ads, no external links in child surfaces, no open chat.
2. **Offline-first**: everything plays with no network; progress is recorded locally.
3. **Localization**: full UI + content in EN/SW/FR with one-click switching.
   The English overlay is not shipped — it was extracted from the content library,
   so every value in it was a string the app already carried; `ContentI18n` resolves
   English straight from the library instead. A picture's `alt` is localized too:
   it is read aloud by a screen reader *and* printed on screen wherever art has not
   been drawn, and until the briefs are translated it falls back to the localized
   narration rather than to the English brief.
4. **No dark patterns**: no timers, no streak-loss shaming, coins buy looks and never lessons.

Aimed at, with real work outstanding:

5. **Accessibility** (WCAG 2.2 AA): captions, text scaling that stacks on the OS setting,
   high contrast, reduced motion, a reading-ease text style, screen-reader labels and a
   coverage test all exist. No external audit has been done, and the reading-ease style
   adjusts spacing rather than shipping a licensed dyslexia typeface.
6. **Compliance** (COPPA / GDPR-K / FERPA, Apple Kids, Play Families): the consent flow, a
   local consent record (`consentPolicyVersion`), `exportFamilyData()` and
   `eraseAllLearningData()` are built. The server-side consent ledger in `backend/` is not
   deployed, and no legal review has been done.
7. **Content moderation before publish**: no CMS or review workflow exists — content is
   authored directly into the library JSON.
