# Watoto — Image / Art Production Guide

Three files (regenerated by `build_image_manifest.py`):

- `image_manifest.csv` — one row per unique image, with every item that uses it, the art brief, and a text-risk flag.
- `image_manifest.json` — counts by stage and usage, plus the text-risk and reuse tallies.
- `IMAGE_README.md` — this guide.

## Scope

543 unique images across all ten stages (about 47–70 per stage). These come from two places in the content: in-lesson/quiz art (`assets[]` images, 383 usages) and story illustrations (`pages[].illustration`, 171 usages). Nine images are reused by more than one item — render those once. Haptics are tracked separately (2 cues) and are not art.

## Naming

Image paths follow `img/s<NN>/<name>.png`, grouped by stage, matching the refs already baked into the content. Deliver each asset to the exact `image_ref` path so the app finds it with no remapping. After any content change, re-run the generator; only new refs need new art (diff the `image_ref` column).

## One render serves all three languages — keep text out of the art

Unlike audio, art is language-neutral: a single illustration works for English, French, and Swahili **as long as no words are baked into the picture**. The app draws localized captions and labels over or beside the image, sourced from the same i18n keys.

The `text_risk` column flags 59 images whose brief implies embedded letters, words, or labels — phonics and word-study art, sight words, and labeled diagrams (e.g. a labeled rectangle, a world map with place names, a fraction bar with values). For these, either keep the lettering out of the asset and let the app overlay localized text, or supply a per-locale variant. Where the letters themselves are the lesson — for instance art that must show the word "cupcake" or the digraph "sh" — treat that text as a separate overlay layer, not paint, so the French and Swahili builds can swap it. This mirrors the `review_for_locale` flag in the audio manifest.

## Style and representation

Follow the curriculum's universal rules: diverse, inclusive characters across skin tones, abilities, and family types; warm and child-safe throughout; no ads, brand marks, or licensed characters. Hold a consistent art direction within each stage, and let it mature with the design tiers — softer and simpler for the infant/toddler stages, more detailed and confident for the older primary stages. Favor a clear focal subject and high contrast for legibility and accessibility.

The `art_brief` column is also the basis for each image's alt text / screen-reader description (most items additionally carry an `accessibility` note), so keep the finished art faithful to its brief — that keeps captions, alt text, and picture in agreement.

## Workflow

1. Produce art from the briefs, text-free wherever `text_risk` is flagged.
2. Deliver to the exact `image_ref` paths.
3. Re-run `build_image_manifest.py` after content edits; only changed refs need new art.

Together with the audio package, art and voice now share one key-driven pipeline: every item's picture, sound, caption, and text all resolve from the same stage/subject/type identity.
